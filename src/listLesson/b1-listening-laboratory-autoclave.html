Listening Transcript

Head Technician: "Good morning, team. Before we begin today's shift in the pathology department, we must review the standardized procedure for using the autoclave. Following these steps in sequence is mandatory for effective STERILIZATION and your personal safety."

Head Technician: "First, check that the wastewater container is empty and the water level in the main chamber is at the Fill line. Second, place your medical instruments into the designated metal baskets. Ensure they are not overlapping, as steam needs to reach every surface. Third, place the baskets inside and turn the handle clockwise to SEAL the door tightly. You must confirm that the safety latch is engaged."

Head Technician: "Next, select the Solid Cycle on the control panel. The temperature will reach 121 degrees Celsius and the pressure will rise. Do not attempt to open the door until the pressure gauge reads zero and the Cycle Complete INDICATOR light turns green. This PRECAUTION prevents severe steam burns. Finally, use heat resistant gloves to UNLOAD the baskets and place the instruments in the cooling zone. Please record the date and time in the equipment logbook immediately after you finish. If you encounter an error code, DISPOSE of any contaminated water and notify the head technician."

Intern: "Understood. We will verify the water levels first and ensure everything is documented."

Task 9 Case Study Decision Making

Scenario:

You are the only person in the lab. The Cycle Complete light is green, but the pressure gauge still shows a small amount of pressure. Your supervisor told you that the gauge is sometimes sticky and inaccurate. You need the instruments immediately for an urgent biopsy.

Option A: Wait until the gauge settles at zero, even if it delays the biopsy.
Option B: Trust the indicator light and carefully crack the door open.

Discuss which choice aligns with the professional standards of GSE 50 for medical professionals.

Analysis:

Option A is the only responsible choice. Safety protocols cannot be bypassed based on assumptions about faulty equipment. Opening a pressurized autoclave can cause severe injury. Communicating the delay to the surgical team is the professional action required here.
