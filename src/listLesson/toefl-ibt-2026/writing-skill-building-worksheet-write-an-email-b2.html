Think about the last time you contacted a customer service department. If you just complained (the problem) without saying what you wanted them to do (the solution), the interaction was probably frustrating.

In academic and professional environments, the recipient of your email is busy. They need to know immediately why you are writing (Purpose), what went wrong (Problem), and how you want to fix it (Solution). Mastering this logical flow guarantees a high score on this writing task.

Purpose, Problem, Solution

Divide your email into three distinct sections to ensure maximum clarity and politeness:

1. Purpose (Opening)

After your greeting, state your reason for writing in the very first sentence. Use phrases like:
"I am writing to inquire about..." or "I am contacting you regarding..."

2. Problem (Context)

Explain the issue politely. Do not sound angry. Use softeners:
"There seems to be an oversight..." or "Unfortunately, I encountered an issue with..."

3. Solution (Action)

Suggest a way to fix the problem using modal verbs (would, could).
"Would it be possible to..." or "Could we arrange an alternative..."

Remember the Closing!

Always finish with a polite sign off that anticipates a response, such as "Thank you for your assistance in resolving this matter." followed by "Sincerely," and your name.

Prompt Scenario

You are a student in History 101. Your professor, Dr. Allen, posted the final grades online. You noticed that you received a "C," but you calculated your points and believe you should have received a "B." Write an email to Dr. Allen asking him to check your grade.

Dear Dr. Allen,

I am writing to you regarding the final grades for History 101 that were recently posted online.

After reviewing my scores for all assignments and exams, there seems to be an oversight. My calculated total points place me in the "B" range, but the system shows my final grade as a "C."

Would it be possible for you to review my scores to verify if an error occurred during data entry? I have attached my personal calculation sheet for your reference.

Thank you for your time and assistance.

Sincerely,
Alex Carter

Purpose Problem Solution
Explanation:

Saying "you made a mistake" is aggressive and places direct blame on the professor. "There seems to be an oversight" uses passive, polite language (a "softener"). It addresses the problem objectively without being disrespectful.
