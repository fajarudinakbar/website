Introduction to the 2026 Writing Format

The updated TOEFL iBT Writing section reflects the digital communication needs of a modern university, moving away from long-form essays to three distinct tasks:

Build a Sentence

Use a "Word Bank" to form a grammatically correct sentence. There can be one extra distractor word.

Write an Email

Respond to a specific campus situation using an appropriate formal register.

Academic Discussion

Contribute to an online classroom forum (approx. 100 words), engaging with the professor and peers.

The 'Hook': Syntactic Complexity

The core of B2-level writing is the ability to move from simple sentences to complex ones that show relationships (cause, contrast, condition).

In the "Build a Sentence" task, the test specifically checks if you can use subordinating conjunctions and connectors (like because, although, or relative pronouns like which) to link ideas accurately, moving beyond basic Subject-Verb-Object structures.

Task: Build a Sentence

Prompt: Why did the committee reject the proposal?

because was the proposal rejected it lacked research sufficient although

Final Constructed Sentence:

"The proposal was rejected because it lacked sufficient research."

Think-Aloud Logic:

  1. Start with the subject: The question asks about the proposal. We assemble: "The proposal was rejected..."
  2. Add the connector: It asks "Why?". We need: "...because..."
  3. Complete the reason: "...it lacked sufficient research."
  4. Identify the extra word: "although" is the distractor. It suggests a contrast that doesn't exist in our logical sequence.

Part B: Write an Email (Register)

Rewrite these informal sentences to fit a formal email to a University Registrar or Professor.

"I need my transcript right now for a job."

Model: "I am writing to request a copy of my official transcript for a job application."

"You guys made a mistake on my bill."

Model: "I believe there may be an error regarding my recent billing statement."

"Tell me when the office opens."

Model: "Could you please inform me of the office's current hours of operation?"

1. "I agree with Paul's point   I would add that technology also lowers costs."

2. "While Sarah argues for more tests,   I believe project-based learning is better."

3. "In contrast to James,   feel that the government should not regulate the internet."

Feedback

Part A and B models have been revealed in their respective sections above.

Correct transitions for Part C:
1. B: and (Adds an extension to the agreement) 2. B: nonetheless ("While" already sets up the contrast; "however" is redundant grammatically in this clause structure) 3. B: I (Provides the necessary subject for the main clause)

Task 2

Write an Email

Context: You are a student in an Economics class. You need to miss the next session because you have a mandatory orientation for a new internship.

Your Task: Write a formal email to your professor, Dr. Hsieh, explaining the situation and asking how to get the materials.
Model Answer:

"Dear Dr. Hsieh,

I am writing to inform you that I will be unable to attend our next Economics class due to a mandatory internship orientation. I am very interested in the topic we will be covering, so could you please let me know if the lecture slides or any supplemental materials will be available online?

Thank you for your understanding."

Task 3

Academic Discussion

Professor:

"Many cities are considering making all public transportation free to reduce traffic and pollution. Do you think this is a good use of tax money, or should the money be spent on other services? Why?"

Student 1 (Alice):

"I support this. Free transit encourages people to leave their cars at home, which directly cuts down on emissions..."

Student 2 (Bob):

"I disagree. If transit is free, the system will lose the money it needs for crucial maintenance and repairs..."

Your Task: Write a response (approx. 100 words). Express your opinion and reference the points made by Alice or Bob.

Model Response:

"While Alice makes a compelling case for the environmental benefits of free transit, I find myself agreeing more with Bob’s concerns regarding maintenance. In my view, the most effective way to reduce traffic is not necessarily to make transit free, but to make it more reliable and frequent. Most commuters value their time more than a small fare. If the government uses tax money to eliminate fares, they might lack the budget to expand routes to underserved areas. Therefore, I believe tax revenue should be invested in improving the infrastructure of the system rather than making it entirely free."

1. Sentence Logic: When building the sentence, did you double-check that every word from the bank (except one) was used?

2. Email Tone: Did you use formal openers like "I am writing to..." or "I would like to..." to keep the tone professional?

3. Discussion Strategy: Did you explicitly mention Alice or Bob's perspective before giving your own specific example?
