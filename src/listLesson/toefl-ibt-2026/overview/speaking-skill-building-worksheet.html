TOEFL iBT Speaking Overview

Skill Focus: Working Memory, Intelligible Delivery, and Logical Elaboration

11 Questions

Important Note: On the real test, there are NO transcripts. Listen to the audio first before revealing the text. The microphone feature uses your browser's speech recognition to transcribe your practice responses.

Structure of the Speaking Section

1. Listen and Repeat (Questions 1 to 7)

  • You hear 7 sentences related to campus or daily life.
  • Sentences increase in length and difficulty.
  • Task: Repeat exactly what you heard after the short pause.

2. Take an Interview (Questions 8 to 11)

  • You participate in a simulated interview (video).
  • The interviewer asks 4 questions about everyday topics.
  • Task: Speak for 45s per question. No preparation time.

A. Working Memory & Intonation

In "Listen and Repeat", you must remember the stress pattern. Read these aloud and note how meaning changes with stress:

"I didn't say we should go to the library."

Meaning: Someone else said it, not me.

"I didn't say we should go to the library."

Meaning: I said we should go somewhere else (e.g., the park).

Task Type: Take an Interview
Interviewer Question

"How do you think technology has changed the way students conduct research?"

Model Response (B2 Level):

"Technology has fundamentally shifted research from physical libraries to digital databases. In the past, students had to spend hours searching through book stacks. However, today we can access thousands of peer-reviewed articles instantly via the internet. For instance, last semester, I completed an entire bibliography for my history paper without ever leaving my dorm room, which saved me a significant amount of time."

Think-Aloud Logic:

  • Direct Answer: State the main change (Physical to Digital).
  • Comparison: Use "In the past" vs. "Today" to show depth.
  • Specific Example: Mention a personal experience ("last semester," "history paper").
  • Connectors: Use words like however and for instance.

Part B: Take an Interview (Elaboration)

Draft a 2-sentence elaboration using the [ Answer + Detail ] framework.

1. "What is your favorite subject at university?"

2. "Do you think online classes are as effective as in-person ones?"

Model Responses

1. Favorite Subject:

"My favorite subject is Environmental Science because it addresses urgent global issues. For example, learning about renewable energy helps me understand how we can reduce our carbon footprint."

2. Online vs In-Person:

"I believe in-person classes are generally more effective because they facilitate immediate interaction. In a physical classroom, it is much easier to ask the professor a question and get instant feedback."

Context: Education and Career Goals interview with a campus counselor.

Question 8
Interviewer Question

"Can you tell me about a specific project or assignment you completed recently that you are proud of?"

Model Answer:

"I am particularly proud of a group presentation I did for my Sociology class last month. We had to analyze local demographic trends, and I was responsible for the data visualization. It was challenging to make the complex data easy to understand, but our professor specifically praised our charts for their clarity, which made me feel very accomplished."

45s
Question 9
Interviewer Question

"In your opinion, what is the most important skill a student needs to be successful in the modern workplace?"

Model Answer:

"I believe the most important skill today is adaptability. The workplace is changing so rapidly due to AI and new software, so students must be able to learn new tools quickly. For instance, in my marketing class, we had to switch to a new data analysis program mid-semester, and those who adapted the fastest were the ones who produced the best final reports."

45s
Question 10
Interviewer Question

"Some students prefer to work part time while studying, while others focus entirely on their academics. Which approach do you think is better?"

Model Answer:

"I think it is better for students to focus entirely on their academics if they can afford to. University coursework is very demanding, and working part time can often lead to burnout or lower grades. For example, my friend tried to work 20 hours a week last semester, and he ended up failing two classes because he didn't have enough time to study."

45s
Question 11
Interviewer Question

"Finally, where do you see yourself professionally five years from now?"

Model Answer:

"In five years, I hope to be working as a junior project manager in the renewable energy sector. By that time, I will have completed my degree and gained some practical field experience. I also plan to have obtained my PMP certification, which will allow me to lead larger teams and contribute to impactful sustainability projects."

45s

1. Fluency: Did you have any long pauses (more than 3 seconds) while thinking of what to say?

2. Elaboration: For the interview questions (8 to 11), did you provide a specific example for at least two of your answers?

3. Listen & Repeat: Were you able to repeat the longer sentences without looking at the text?
