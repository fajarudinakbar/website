Introduction to the 2026 Reading Format

The updated TOEFL iBT Reading section reflects the "new literacy" required for modern academic success. It tests your ability to process English in three distinct ways:

  • Complete the Words (C-Test): Measures lexical and grammatical competence. You must reconstruct words in a paragraph where the second half of every second word is deleted.
  • Read in Daily Life: Involves "non-linear" reading (flyers, menus, maps). Tests finding specific info quickly in a functional context.
  • Read an Academic Passage: Short, formal articles testing main ideas, details, and author's purpose.

The 'Hook': Awareness

In the 2026 format, reading is an active search for meaning rather than a passive scan. You need to adjust your reading speed and focus based on the task.

Text A (Scanning)

"The seminar is in Room 402, not Room 405. Please arrive ten minutes early for registration."

Text B (Parsing / C-Test)

"The sem____ is i_ Room 402, n__ Room 405."

Self-Learning Insight: If you are reading Text A, you are looking for a location. If you are completing Text B, you are using your knowledge of English spelling and grammar to fill in the missing pieces. The 2026 test asks you to do both.

Strategy: Solving the C-Test

The "Half-Word" Rule: If a word has an even number of letters (e.g., HELP), the first half is given (HE) and you provide the second half (LP). If odd (e.g., STUDY), the first part given is longer (STU) and you provide the rest (DY).

"The stud had t finish th project b Monday."

Think-Aloud Logic:

  • stud____: Starts with "stud." Context: a person in a school. Likely students.
  • t_: Follows "had." This is the infinitive marker to.
  • th_: A common article. Likely the.
  • b_: A preposition indicating a deadline. Likely by.

Part B: Daily Life & Academic Logic

Daily Life

A poster says: "Free Yoga Class: Tuesday @ 5 PM in the Gym. Bring your own mat."
Question: Can a student attend if they don't have a mat?

Daily Life

An email subject: "REVISED: Lab hours for Chemistry 101."
Question: What is the likely purpose of this email?

Daily Life

A campus map shows "Building C" between the Library and the Cafeteria.
Question: Where is Building C?

Academic

"While solar power is renewable, the initial cost of installing panels remains high."
Question: What is the main disadvantage of solar power mentioned?

Academic

"The term 'urban heat island' refers to cities being warmer than rural areas due to human activity."
Question: What causes the temperature difference in cities?

Answer Key & Explanations
Part A (C-Test):
  • people, that, technology, changing, lives
  • professor, the, to, their
  • need, find, to, energy
  • library, until, during, finals
  • is, to, balanced, for, health
Part B (Logic):
  • 6. No: The poster explicitly says "Bring your own mat," meaning mats are not provided.
  • 7. To inform/update: "REVISED" implies a change to the schedule.
  • 8. Between library/cafeteria: This is a spatial reasoning task based on the description.
  • 9. High initial cost: The text uses "While" to contrast the benefit (renewable) with the cost (disadvantage).
  • 10. Human activity: The text explicitly attributes the warmth to "human activity."

Task 3 Read an Academic Passage (Social Science)

"Social identity theory suggests that an individual's sense of who they are is based on their group membership. People naturally categorize themselves into 'in-groups' and 'out-groups.' This categorization often leads to 'in-group favoritism,' where individuals prefer members of their own group over others. While this can build strong community bonds, it can also lead to prejudice against those outside the group."

Question: What is a potential negative consequence of social categorization?

Answer Key & Explanations
Task 1 (C-Test):

"Maps are tools that help us understand the world around us. They show the locations of places like cities, rivers, and mountains. These visual guides can also display different types of information, such as climate or population. There are many kinds, including physical, political, and thematic versions. The study of maps and cartography, the process by which they are made, can teach us about the geography of our planet and how people live in different regions."

Task 2 (Daily Life):

Correct: B Residents can still use the elevators.

Reasoning: The text explicitly states "Elevators will remain operational," which directly supports option B.

Task 3 (Academic):

Correct: C Prejudice against individuals outside the group.

Reasoning: The passage states that while categorization builds bonds, "it can also lead to prejudice against those outside the group."
