Introduction to the 2026 Listening Format

The updated TOEFL iBT Listening section measures your ability to understand spoken English as it is used in colleges and universities. You will encounter:

  • Listen and Choose a Response: Select the most appropriate socially oriented response to a short prompt.
  • Listen to a Conversation: Dialogue between two speakers regarding dining, education, or travel.
  • Listen to an Announcement: Short academic or campus related messages.
  • Listen to an Academic Talk: Short lecture on topics like history, science, or psychology.

The Hook: Awareness

Recognizing speaker roles, purposes, and implied meanings is crucial. Listen to the transcript below and infer the correct meaning.

Noticing Activity

Audio Track 1

Woman: Need anything from the supermarket?
Man: Huh? Aren't we getting ready to go see that play in a few minutes?
Woman: That's tomorrow.
Man: Oh. Wow, I'd forget my head if it wasn't screwed on...

Question: What is the man's implied problem?

Answer & Insight

Correct: B
The man uses a formulaic idiom ("forget my head") to admit he was confused about the date and is forgetful, not that he is experiencing physical pain.

Task: Listen and Choose a Response

In this task, you must select the response that best fits the social context of the prompt naturally.

Audio Prompt

"Didn't I just see you in the library an hour ago?"

Logic Breakdown

Correct: A
It naturally confirms the speaker's observation ("As a matter of fact") and adds a relevant detail explaining why they were there ("returning a book"). Option B answers a "Where" question, and Option C answers a request.

Part 4 Academic Talk (Psychology)

Lecture

"Let's consider 'Directed Attention Fatigue.' This happens when we use our 'hard fascination', that intense, effortful focus, for too long without a break. When your brain is on overload, you lose the ability to inhibit distractions. This is why you might feel irritable or find it impossible to stay on task after a long day of screen time. To recover, you need 'soft fascination', like staring at a sunset or walking in nature, which allows your directed attention to rest."

Question 1: What causes "mental fatigue"?

Question 2: What is a symptom mentioned?

Answer Key & Explanations
Part 1:

Correct: B

Reasoning: Directly addresses the plan/status of the application by giving a relevant reason why it is not happening.

Part 2:

Correct Idea: Change their topic to the city's water system.

Reasoning: The professor confirms that focusing on the city's water system "would be perfect" and the student previously mentioned they should switch to it.

Part 3:

Correct: B A temporary change in parking locations.

Reasoning: The announcement informs students that they must park in a different lot (West lot instead of North lot) temporarily.

Part 4:

Q1 Correct Idea: Intense focus or 'hard fascination' for too long.

Q2 Correct Idea: Irritability, stress, or inability to focus.
