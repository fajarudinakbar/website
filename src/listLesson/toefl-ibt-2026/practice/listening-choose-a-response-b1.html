Choose a Response (B1 Level)

Topic: Everyday Campus Life | Skill: Pragmatic Comprehension

Welcome! In the communicative Choose a Response listening task, you will hear a short spoken utterance, such as a question, request, or statement, and you must quickly select the most natural and pragmatically appropriate response. This worksheet focuses on B1 level campus interactions. You have a maximum of 2 attempts per exercise. Transcripts are hidden by default to build true listening skills! Let's begin.

In your native language, if someone says, "It's freezing in here," they aren't usually asking for a weather report. They are making a polite request for you to close the window or turn up the heat. This is called a Speech Act, the hidden purpose behind the words.

To master the "Choose a Response" task, you cannot just translate words; you must translate the speaker's intent (e.g., Are they offering help? Apologizing? Asking for directions?).

Metacognitive Question: Think about your daily interactions. How does your response change if a professor asks "Do you have a minute?" versus if your best friend asks the same question?

Let's look at one example with "Think Aloud" logic to understand how to eliminate wrong answers.

1

Audio Prompt

Transcript: "Would you mind keeping the noise down a bit?"

(A) Yes, I enjoy loud music. ✗ Incorrect (Rude/Literal misinterpretation)
(B) Oh, I'm sorry, I didn't realize I was being loud. ✓ Correct
(C) No, the noise is over there. ✗ Incorrect (Irrelevant)
(D) Yes, please turn it up. ✗ Incorrect (Opposite meaning)

Explanation

The phrase "Would you mind..." is a polite request to stop doing something.

Option A answers "Yes" (meaning "Yes, I mind") but then contradicts it.
Option B apologizes and complies, which is the socially appropriate response to a complaint.
