The "A.C.E." Argument Structure

To score a 5/5 on this task, you must move beyond basic opinions. Use the A.C.E. structure to build a compelling 100+ word response:

1. Acknowledge (The Hook)

Do not just start with "I think." Start by synthesizing the ongoing debate.

"While David raises a valid concern regarding inflation, I strongly align with Sarah's perspective that..."
2. Contribute (The Pivot)

Introduce a completely new supporting idea that neither student mentioned.

"Furthermore, implementing this policy would uniquely catalyze..."
3. Elaborate (The Anchor)

Provide a concrete, real world example to substantiate your new idea. End with a strong concluding thought showing the broader implications.

Professor Reynolds (Economics)

As automation and artificial intelligence continue to displace traditional jobs, many economists propose implementing a Universal Basic Income (UBI), a guaranteed, unconditional monthly payment from the government to all citizens. Proponents argue it eradicates poverty and fosters innovation, while critics warn of severe economic repercussions. For our discussion today, I want you to consider: Should governments implement a Universal Basic Income to combat the economic effects of automation? Why or why not?

Sarah

I absolutely believe UBI is necessary. As AI takes over administrative and manufacturing sectors, millions will be left without income. A UBI provides a crucial safety net. Furthermore, if people aren't constantly stressed about basic survival, they have the freedom to pursue education, start small businesses, or engage in creative work, which ultimately benefits the economy.

David

I disagree with implementing UBI. Pragmatically, funding such a massive program would require astronomical tax increases, crippling the middle class. More importantly, providing unconditional money disincentivizes hard work. If individuals receive a comfortable living wage without working, we will likely see a massive drop in labor force participation, which would stagnate national productivity.

Example:

You could argue that human psychology is driven by more than just basic survival. Even with a UBI covering basic food and rent, humans inherently desire status, luxury goods, and purpose, ensuring they will continue to seek employment to afford a higher standard of living.

With the rapid advancement of online learning platforms, some university administrators are proposing that traditional, synchronous lectures be completely replaced by asynchronous, pre-recorded modules. This would allow students to learn at their own pace and reduce campus overhead costs. However, critics argue this destroys the university experience. Should universities fully transition to asynchronous, pre-recorded lectures? Why or why not?

Michael

I think it's a terrible idea. Pre-recorded videos are incredibly boring. Without a professor physically in the room, it's impossible to ask spontaneous questions or have lively debates. Learning requires human interaction, not just staring at a screen.

Emma

I support the transition. The flexibility of asynchronous learning is unmatched. Many students work part time or have family obligations, and forcing them to attend a rigid schedule is outdated. If they have questions, they can simply email the professor or use a discussion board.
