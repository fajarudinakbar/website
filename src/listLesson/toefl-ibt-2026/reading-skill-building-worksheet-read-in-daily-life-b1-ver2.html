Read in Daily Life (B1 Level)

Topic: Campus Notices & Schedules | Skill: Information Retrieval

Welcome! In the updated TOEFL iBT® Reading section, the Read in Daily Life task measures your ability to understand practical, non academic texts. At the B1 level, these texts include short emails, campus announcements, event flyers, and simple rules. You have a maximum of 2 attempts per exercise before correct answers and explanations are revealed. Let's begin!

When you look at a bus schedule, you don't read every single word from top to bottom. You scan for the specific time and destination you need. When you read a warning sign, you look for the main instruction (like "Do Not Enter").

The Read in Daily Life task tests this exact skill. You must switch your brain from "academic reading" (reading slowly to understand deep concepts) to "practical reading" (scanning quickly to find facts, rules, or the sender's main purpose).

Metacognitive Question: Think about the last email you received from your school or workplace. Did you read every sentence, or did you skim it to answer the question: "What do I need to do?"

These practical words frequently appear in university emails, signs, and instructions. Match the 15 terms to their correct definitions. (2 Attempts)

How to Beat "Daily Life" Reading Texts

When you encounter a notice or email on the test, use this 3 step strategy:

1. Read the Question First

Do not read the text yet. Read the question to find out exactly what you are looking for. Are you looking for a date? A rule? A reason?

2. Scan for Keywords

If the question asks "What happens if you are late?", scan the text for words like "late," "after," "penalty," or "cannot enter."

3. Watch for Exceptions

Pay attention to conditional words like if, unless, only, and except. These words change the meaning of the rules.

Read this email sent to university students. We will use this text for the strategy check.

To: All Students

From: University IT Services

Subject: Important: Email System Update

Dear Students,

We are updating the university email system this weekend to improve security. The system will be offline from Saturday at 10:00 PM until Sunday at 6:00 AM.

During this time, you will not be able to send or receive emails. Please make sure to download any important documents you need for your weekend homework before the update begins.

On Sunday morning, you must log in and create a new password. Your old password will no longer work.

Thank you for your cooperation.

Explanation:

You need to scan for the keyword "Sunday morning". The text states: "On Sunday morning, you must log in and create a new password."

Main Library: Borrowing Policies

All university students can borrow books from the main library using their student ID card. Please read the rules below carefully to avoid fines.

Borrowing Limits and Times
  • Undergraduate Students: May borrow up to 10 books at a time for a period of 14 days.
  • Graduate Students: May borrow up to 20 books at a time for a period of 30 days.
  • Course Reserves: Books marked "Course Reserve" can only be borrowed for 2 hours and must stay inside the library building.
Late Fines and Renewals

If you do not return a book on time, you will be charged a late fee of $1.00 per day. If a book is more than 30 days late, it is considered lost, and you must pay $50.00 to replace it.

You can renew a book online to get more time. However, you cannot renew a book if another student has requested it.

Campus Dining: Meal Plan Options

Welcome to Campus Dining! All students living in campus dormitories must choose one of the following meal plans for the semester:

  • The Gold Plan: 21 meals per week. Best for students who eat all their meals on campus. Cost: $1,500/semester.

Score Goal: 5/5

Notice: Changes to Campus Shuttle Routes

Starting next Monday, November 1st, there will be changes to the campus shuttle bus schedule due to road construction on West Avenue.

Route A (Red Line): This bus normally travels from the Main Library to the Science Building every 15 minutes. During the construction, it will take a longer route and arrive every 25 minutes. Please plan your travel time accordingly.

Route B (Blue Line): This route will not change. It will continue to run from the student dormitories to the downtown shopping center every 30 minutes.

Weekend Service: Shuttle service on Saturdays and Sundays will end at 8:00 PM instead of 10:00 PM until the construction is completely finished.

To ride any campus shuttle, students must show their student ID to the driver. Guests can ride for a fee of $2.00 per trip.
