At the B2 (Upper Intermediate) level, you must navigate formal vocabulary, recognize conditional rules (exceptions), and infer the reasons behind institutional changes.

Have you ever heard an announcement at an airport that sounded like this? "All flights are boarding on time, with the exception of Flight 402 to London, which is delayed until 8:00 PM due to weather."

At a B2 level, test makers are not just testing if you know the flight is delayed. They are testing if you caught the exception. Campus announcements on the TOEFL function the same way. They outline a general rule and then provide a specific condition, deadline, or exception. Your job is to listen for the administrative "fine print."

The Four Pillars of B2 Announcements

Most university announcements follow a predictable, logical structure. Train your brain to listen for these four pillars:

1. The Core Update

What is the main news? (e.g., "The library will be renovated.") Usually stated in the first 10 seconds.

2. The Rationale

Why is this happening? Listen for "due to," "as a result of," or "in order to." Questions often ask for this reason.

3. The Exception

Who is not affected? Listen for "exempt," "unless," or "prior to." This is where the trick questions hide.

4. The Call to Action

What must the listener do next? (e.g., "Please submit your forms online.") This usually concludes the message.
