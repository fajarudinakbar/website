TOEFL iBT Skill-Building Worksheet

Section: Reading | Task Type: Read in Daily Life (Campus & Workplace)

Target Level: C1 | Skill Focus: Pragmatic Competence & Tone Deciphering

Step 1: Schema Activation (The Hook)

In professional environments, messages are rarely just about facts. Consider this memo:

"While the current workflow has served us adequately, we are exploring more streamlined alternatives to optimize our output."

Reflect: Is the department head happy? The word "adequately" often implies "barely acceptable." Decoding this subtext is crucial for C1 success.

Step 2: Strategy Definition

At the C1 level, you must navigate hedging (cautious language) and identify the writer’s attitude.

Pro Tip: Look for "Qualifier Words"

Adverbs like nominally, ostensibly, or reportedly often signal that the writer is distancing themselves from the statement or implying that reality differs from appearance.

Step 3: Noticing Activity

Compare these two snippets communicating the same literal message.

Option A: "The system is down for repairs. It will be back online tomorrow. Use the backup server until then."
Option B: "Due to unforeseen technical constraints, the primary interface is currently undergoing a period of maintenance. It is anticipated that full functionality will be restored by tomorrow. In the interim, users are encouraged to utilize the secondary server."

Which option uses "hedging" / indirect language?

What does the tone of Option B suggest about the nature of the issue?

Step 4: Modeling & Think-Aloud

Office of Registrar Notice

"In light of the recent surge in cross-departmental enrollments, the University has decided to implement a revised 'Prerequisite Waiver' protocol, effective next semester. While the previous policy allowed for discretionary approval by individual faculty members, the new framework necessitates a centralized review. This measure is intended to ensure parity across all disciplines, though we acknowledge it may introduce a slight delay."
Think-Aloud (Purpose): Why the change? The text says "to ensure parity" (fairness).
Think-Aloud (The Catch): "Necessitates centralized review" implies professors are losing their power to personally approve students. "Slight delay" is likely a euphemism for bureaucracy.

Step 5: Linguistic Scaffolding (Power Phrases)

Master these C1 connectors and idiomatic structures.

"Notwithstanding [X]..." (In spite of/Despite)
"To be held in abeyance" (Temporarily suspended)
"Under the auspices of..." (With support/patronage of)
"A point of contention" (Disagreement)
"In the interim" (Meanwhile)
"Contingent upon..." (Depending on)
"To exercise discretion" (Make personal judgment)

Step 6: Controlled Micro-Practice

Replace the bolded informal phrases with a C1 Power Phrase from Step 5.

1. The project is on hold [ ... ] until we get more funding.

2. Your bonus is depending on [ ... ] your performance review.

3. Even with [ ... ] the recent rain, the city still has a water shortage.

4. The event was organized by [ ... ] the Student Union.

5. Students may choose for themselves [ ... ] whether to attend.

Step 7: Guided Macro-Practice

Scenario: You are a project manager. A deadline was missed because a vendor was late. Tell the Board without making excuses, but hold the vendor accountable. Use the Power Phrases.

Step 8: Full-Task Simulation

Excerpt: Corporate Sustainability Report

"The transition to a 'Paperless Initiative' has yielded significant environmental dividends; however, the transition has not been without its logistical hurdles. Feedback suggests that certain departments feel the digital migration was somewhat precipitated, leading to gaps in archival accessibility. Consequently, the Executive Committee has authorized a 'Retroactive Digitization' phase. This is not to be construed as a return to physical record-keeping, but rather as a refinement of our existing digital repositories to ensure no data remains siloed."

1. What is the author's primary objective in this passage?

2. Based on the passage, what can be inferred about the "digital migration"?

3. What does the author imply by saying "not to be construed as a return to physical record-keeping"?
