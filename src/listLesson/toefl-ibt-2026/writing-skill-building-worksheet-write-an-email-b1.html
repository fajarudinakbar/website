Writing an email in a university setting is different from texting a friend. You must consider two key factors: Purpose (what you need to achieve, like asking a question or solving a problem) and Tone (how formal or informal you should be).

If you are writing to a professor to ask for a deadline extension, your tone should be formal and polite. If you are replying to a classmate about a study group, your tone can be more relaxed. The Write an Email task specifically tests your ability to match your writing to the situation.

How to Structure a Winning Email

A good response in this task is clear, direct, and well organized. Always use this simple 3 part structure:

1. The Opening

Start with an appropriate greeting (e.g., "Dear Mr. Davis," or "Hi Sarah,"). Then, explicitly state the reason for your email in the first sentence.

2. The Body

Address the prompt directly. Provide the requested information, ask your question, or offer a solution. Keep it concise but ensure you answer all parts of the original message.

3. The Closing

End with a polite closing statement (e.g., "Thank you for your help," or "I look forward to hearing from you.") followed by a sign off (e.g., "Sincerely," or "Best,") and your name.

Avoid Wordiness

This is an email, not an essay. The goal is clear communication. Do not use overly complex vocabulary if it makes the message confusing. Aim for about 50 to 100 words.

Read the email prompt below carefully. Notice who the sender is and what action they are requesting.

From: Housing Office <housing@university.edu>

To: All North Hall Residents

Subject: Upcoming Maintenance Schedule

Dear Residents,

We will be performing routine maintenance on the heating systems in North Hall next week. We need access to every room for approximately 30 minutes.

Please reply to this email by Friday and let us know which day (Tuesday, Wednesday, or Thursday) and time preference (morning or afternoon) works best for you. Someone must be present in the room during the maintenance.

Thank you,

The Housing Office

Explanation:

To successfully answer the prompt, your reply must explicitly state: 1) A preferred day (Tuesday, Wednesday, or Thursday) and 2) A preferred time of day (morning or afternoon).
