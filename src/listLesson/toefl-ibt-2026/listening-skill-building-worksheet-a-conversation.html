Listen to a Conversation (B1 Level)

Topic: Campus Life & Services | Skill: Main Ideas & Details

Welcome! In the TOEFL iBT® Listening section, you will hear conversations between a student and a professor, or a student and a campus worker. At the B1 (Intermediate) level, the speakers use common words to solve everyday problems.

  • This worksheet uses a Text to Speech Simulator. Listen to the audio before reading the transcript!
  • You have a maximum of 2 attempts per exercise before the correct answers are revealed.

Think about the last time you asked for help at a store or a school office. You started by stating your problem ("I can't find my classroom"). The worker then gave you a solution ("It is on the second floor, to the right").

TOEFL conversations follow this exact pattern. The student usually has a problem, and the professor or worker helps them solve it. If you can answer two simple questions while listening "What is the problem?" and "What is the solution?" you will perform very well on this task.

Types of Conversation Questions

After you listen to a conversation, you will answer questions about it. Here are the three most common types at the B1 level:

1. The Main Purpose

"Why does the student go to see the professor?"
The student will almost always say the reason in their very first or second sentence. Listen closely at the beginning!

2. The Specific Detail

"What does the student need to bring?"
Listen for factual answers given by the worker or professor. Often, it involves a rule, a location, or an item.

3. The Next Action

"What will the student probably do next?"
Listen to the very end of the conversation. They usually agree on a plan (e.g., "Okay, I will fill out the form now").

Audio File

Student: "Hi, I need some help. I received an email saying I have an overdue book, but I'm sure I returned it last week."

Librarian: "Hello. I can check that for you. May I see your student ID card, please?"

Student: "Yes, here it is. My name is David Smith."

Librarian: "Thank you, David. Let me look at the computer... Ah, yes. It says the book 'Modern History' was due on Tuesday."

Student: "That's the one! But I put it in the return box on Monday afternoon."

Librarian: "Oh, I see what happened. Sometimes it takes us a day or two to process books from the return box. Let me update the system for you right now so you won't be charged a late fee."

Student: "Oh, that's great. Thank you so much for fixing it!"

Explanation:

Problem: The student got an email about an overdue book, but he already returned it. Solution: The librarian will update the computer system so the student does not have to pay a late fee.

Professor: "Perfect. Bring the note to class on Monday, along with your finished essay."
