Task Guidelines

How This Task is Evaluated

In the Write for an Academic Discussion task, you are expected to post a contribution to an online class discussion. You must respond directly to the professor's question while integrating, extending, or evaluating the opinions of your classmates. Your response is evaluated on the depth of your ideas, your direct engagement, and your language facility.

The Class Discussion Scenario

P

Professor Dr. Allen

"Over the next few weeks, we will study the impacts of extracurricular community service programs. Some educators argue that mandatory volunteer work builds crucial character in university students, while others believe that forcing service undermines its very value. Should high school or university students be required to complete volunteer hours to graduate? Why or why not?"

S1

Student 1 (Sarah)

Volunteering builds civic responsibility and teaches students the value of helping others. It helps them feel invested in their local neighborhoods.

S2

Student 2 (Takahiro)

Students already have limited free time because many work part-time jobs or help their families with crucial household chores.

Discussion: Extracurricular Obligations
Word Count: 104 words

I think volunteering is helpful, like Student 1 says, because it teaches students to help others. It also lets students see more of their community.

But Student 2 is right that many students are very busy with jobs or family responsibilities, so it can be difficult for them to find extra time for required volunteer hours.

Because both sides make sense, I am not sure that volunteering should be required for every student. Maybe schools can encourage students to volunteer, but it should not be a strict rule because different students have different situations and schedules.

Volunteer work can help students build civic responsibility, as Student 1 suggests, because it exposes them to people and situations they may not encounter in school. For example, volunteering at a food pantry can help students understand issues such as local food insecurity. This experience teaches them how community organizations operate and why support is necessary.

However, Student 2 raises an important concern: many teenagers already manage part-time jobs or care for younger siblings, which reduces the time they can commit to long volunteer shifts.

A good compromise is requiring volunteer hours but allowing students to complete them gradually throughout the year. This approach maintains the benefits of civic engagement while reducing pressure on students with heavy schedules.

Volunteer work can provide valuable learning opportunities, but any requirement must account for the diverse circumstances students face outside school. Student 1 is correct that meaningful service experiences build civic responsibility; for instance, mentoring younger children or assisting at community shelters helps students see how their time and effort create immediate, positive change. These experiences often increase students’ long-term interest in community engagement.

However, Student 2 highlights an important issue of fairness. Students who work after school or care for family members cannot reasonably complete the same number of hours as classmates with fewer responsibilities.

A tiered requirement would address this problem: students with significant outside obligations could complete fewer but more intensive project-based hours, while others engage in longer-term service. This system ensures that all students participate meaningfully without creating additional barriers or stress.
