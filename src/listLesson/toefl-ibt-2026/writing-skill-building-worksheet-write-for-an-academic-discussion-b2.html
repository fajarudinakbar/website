Imagine walking into a room where two friends are arguing. You would not just shout, "I agree with Sarah!" A sophisticated participant first acknowledges the context of the debate, supports their ally with fresh evidence, and then politely explains exactly why the other friend is incorrect.

This is precisely what the TOEFL graders want to see. You must show the professor that you have read your classmates' opinions, understood them, and can systematically build your own argument while tearing down the opposing view.

The Winning Blueprint (120+ Words)

To ensure a high score, write exactly four paragraphs using this specific structural blueprint:

1. Overview (1 to 2 sentences)

Directly address the professor's question while naturally summarizing both perspectives.
"The administration faces a difficult decision regarding X, with Student A arguing for Y and Student B defending Z."

2. Agreement (4 sentences)

State your side and build it up using four elements: Claim, Reason, Example, Elaboration.
"I strongly agree with Student A... This is because... For example... This demonstrates that..."

3. Disagreement (4 sentences)

Dismantle the opposing side using four elements: Claim, Reason, Example, Elaboration.
"Conversely, Student B's argument is flawed... He fails to consider... For instance... Therefore..."

4. Conclusion (1 to 2 sentences)

A strong closing statement seamlessly tying the two viewpoints together.
"Ultimately, while [Opposing View] has minor benefits, prioritizing [Your View] ensures long-term success."

This week, we are discussing how student performance is evaluated. Traditionally, universities use letter grades (A, B, C, etc.) to show how well a student understands the material. However, some educators propose switching entirely to a "Pass/Fail" system, where students only receive a "Pass" if they meet the minimum requirements, or a "Fail" if they do not. Do you think universities should replace traditional letter grades with a Pass/Fail system? Why or why not?

Sarah

I think switching to a Pass/Fail system is a great idea. University students are under too much pressure to get perfect grades. This constant stress hurts their mental health. If we use a Pass/Fail system, students will focus on actually learning the material and enjoying their classes, rather than just memorizing facts to get an "A".

David

I disagree with Sarah. Traditional letter grades are essential because they provide motivation. If students know they only need a minimum score to "Pass," many will stop trying to do their best work. Furthermore, future employers and graduate schools need letter grades to accurately evaluate a student's skills and performance.

University Budget Discussion

Professor: Due to recent budget cuts, the university must reduce funding for some student activities. The administration is deciding whether to cut funding for university sports teams or for the arts programs (like theater, music, and painting). In your opinion, which program should keep its funding, and which one should be reduced? Why?

Mark: I think sports funding should be protected. University sports bring students together and create a strong sense of community. Plus, big sports games generate money for the university by selling tickets to the public.

Elena: I believe the arts should keep their funding. The arts encourage creativity and cultural understanding, which are essential for a well-rounded education. We already focus too much on competition and not enough on personal expression.

Target: 120+ words
Word Count: 0

Sample Answer (162 words):

The administration faces a difficult decision regarding budget cuts, with Mark defending sports programs and Elena advocating for the arts.

I strongly agree with Elena that arts funding must be protected. This is because creative programs develop essential critical thinking and emotional intelligence skills. For example, participating in a university theater production requires students to collaboratively solve complex logistical problems on a tight budget. Experiencing this type of creative collaboration prepares students for diverse professional environments far better than physical competition does.

Conversely, Mark's argument that sports generate necessary revenue is fundamentally flawed. He fails to consider that the massive maintenance and insurance costs associated with athletic facilities often completely erase any profits from ticket sales. For instance, my previous university spent millions renovating a football stadium, which ultimately resulted in higher tuition fees for everyone. Therefore, prioritizing athletics often creates an unexpected financial burden for the entire student body rather than a sustainable economic benefit.

Ultimately, while sports offer community engagement, prioritizing the arts ensures that the university continues to produce well-rounded, innovative thinkers.

If people are not allowed to drive their cars into the city, it will force everyone to use public transportation or bicycles, which is much better for the environment.

Maria: I disagree with banning cars immediately. It is unfair to people who live outside the city and commute to work, especially if they cannot afford an electric car. The government needs to focus on making public transportation cheaper and more reliable first, before they start banning vehicles.
