Listen to an Academic Talk (B2 Level)

Topic: University Lectures & Concepts | Skill: Note Taking & Comprehension

Welcome! In the TOEFL iBT® Listening section, the Academic Talk task evaluates your ability to comprehend university level lectures. At the B2 level, lectures are 100 to 250 words long. You must accurately capture main ideas, understand supporting details, make inferences, and recognize the organizational features of a talk.

  • This worksheet includes a built in Audio Simulator.
  • Always listen to the audio before reading the transcript.
  • You have a maximum of 2 attempts per exercise before answers are revealed.

Academic listening is not a passive activity; it requires highly active cognitive engagement. When a professor speaks, they naturally emphasize core concepts and use rhetorical signals to indicate transitions (e.g., "Moving on to..." or "A classic example is...").

Your goal is not to transcribe every word, but to build a mental and written outline of the lecture's architecture. Focus on the relationship between the broader theories and the specific, concrete examples the professor uses to illustrate them.

Navigating the Audio

To achieve a Band Score of 4 to 6 (B2 to C2 levels), you must move beyond basic word recognition. Use these strategies:

1. The 30 Second Rule

Lecturers typically state the core topic within the first 30 seconds. Listen carefully to the introduction to anchor your understanding. Ask yourself: "What are we talking about today?"

2. Track the Organization

The questions require you to recognize organizational features. Listen for structural cues like "First," "On the other hand," "For instance," or "As a result."

3. Avoid Sound Traps

Do not select an answer choice simply because it contains a word you heard in the audio. Incorrect options often misuse words directly from the lecture to distract you.

Transcript: "An ecological footprint is a measure of the environmental impact of an individual, community, or country. For instance, the ecological footprint of someone living in a developed country is typically larger than that of someone living in a developing country, mainly due to higher consumption levels and waste production. Factors such as energy use, transportation, food consumption, and housing play significant roles in determining the size of an ecological footprint.

Understanding ecological footprints is crucial for promoting sustainability. It helps identify areas where changes can be made to reduce environmental impact. For example, if you consume locally produced foods, you'll likely reduce your ecological footprint because less energy will be used for transporting that food. Governments and organizations can use ecological footprint data to develop policies aimed at achieving sustainable development. I'll discuss some examples of that next."

Audio: Fascination Types

Transcript: "Did you see that new thriller movie that came out last week? I did and loved it. The action, the plot twists... I was totally captivated. Time just flew by. There's another type of fascination soft fascination. Now, one thing to know is hard fascination causes mental fatigue. The mind is so intensely focused that it gets tired fast. What follows mental fatigue? You might find yourself easily distracted, irritable, and stressed. Soft fascination, in contrast, engages a different part of the brain the DMN, or Default Mode Network, which soothes the mind and helps combat mental fatigue.
