Listen and Repeat (B2 Level)

Topic: Daily Life | Skill: Meaning-Based Repetition

Welcome! In a "Listen and Repeat" task, the goal is not to act like a parrot. If you try to memorize every single word, you will panic and forget the sentence. At the B2 level, your strategy is to understand the core message and deliver the same idea fluently. If you miss a specific word but replace it with a good synonym, you succeed! Use the built-in Audio Player and Transcriber Tool below to practice this essential fluency skill.

Imagine a train station announcement says: "Due to severe weather conditions, all outbound trains have been delayed until further notice."

If you run to tell your friend, you probably won't use those exact words. You will likely say: "The trains are delayed right now because of the bad weather."

This is natural communication! Fluency is about capturing the Subject, Action, and Key Detail. If you obsess over perfect grammar or exact phrasing while listening, your brain will freeze. Listen for the movie playing in your head, then describe the movie.

Metacognitive Question: Why is visualizing an action in your mind (like seeing a calendar date move from Friday to Wednesday) more effective for memory than trying to memorize a list of English words?

How to Process Sentences Instantly

When listening to a complex sentence, immediately break it down into three core chunks. If you remember the chunks, the exact words do not matter.

1. The "Who/What"

Identify the main subject immediately. Is the sentence about the landlord, the train station, or the grocery store?

2. The "Action"

What is happening? Are they canceling, delaying, requiring, or discovering something? Focus on the main verb.

3. The "Key Detail"

Catch the most important limitation. Is there a specific time, location, or reason? (e.g., "by Friday," "due to rain").

Paraphrase if Panicking!

If you hear: "The mandatory seminar will commence precisely at noon." and you forget "commence," just say: "The required seminar will start exactly at noon." You have successfully delivered the message!
