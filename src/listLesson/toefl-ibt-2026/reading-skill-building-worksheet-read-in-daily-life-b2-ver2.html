Read in Daily Life (B2 Level)

Topic: Policies, Conditions, and Formal Campus Notices

Instructions: At the B2 level, "Daily Life" reading moves beyond simple signs and schedules. You must understand university policies, complex instructions, and formal announcements. Pay strict attention to conditions, exceptions, and paraphrase.

Task 1 Cultural Connection

In everyday life, when faced with a long list of "Terms and Conditions", many people simply scroll to the bottom and click "Agree" without reading.

Question: Why will this habit cause you to fail B2 level TOEFL questions, and what specific words should you look for instead?

Strategy: In TOEFL, the questions are designed to test the exceptions hidden in the fine print. You cannot just skim for the general idea. You must actively scan for conditional words like unless, except, provided that, liable, and prior to. The answer is usually in the sentence containing these words.

Task 3 The Exception Trap

Read this rule: "All library books must be returned within 14 days, unless they are marked as 'Reference Only', in which case they cannot leave the building."

Question: Can you take a 'Reference Only' book home for 7 days? Why or why not?

Analysis: No. The word "unless" creates an exception. While normal books have a 14 day limit, 'Reference Only' books have a different rule applied to them ("cannot leave the building"). Therefore, 0 days are allowed outside.

Mastering Administrative English

At the B2 level, you will encounter texts written in Administrative Register. This is the formal style used by universities, businesses, and governments to enforce rules and limit liability.

Key Characteristics to Recognize:

  • Passive Voice: To sound objective, texts use passive structures. Instead of "You must pay a fee," you will read "A fee will be assessed."
  • Conditional Clauses: Sentences often start with "If," "Should you," or "In the event of," setting specific conditions for actions.
  • Nominalization: Verbs are turned into nouns. Instead of "We will cancel your ticket," you will read "This will result in the cancellation of your ticket."

Test Taking Strategy: When a question asks a specific detail (e.g., "What is the penalty for late submission?"), do not read the entire document. Scan for keywords like "late," "penalty," or "fee," and then read that specific sentence very carefully.

Task 4 Strategy Check: Paraphrasing Rules

Original text: "Students are prohibited from entering the laboratory without prior authorization from the instructor."

Which of the following is an accurate paraphrase?

A. Instructors must enter the laboratory before the students.

B. You cannot go into the lab unless a teacher gives you permission first.

Answer: B. "Prohibited" = cannot go. "Prior authorization" = permission first. Option A confuses "prior" (meaning 'before in time or order') with physically entering first.

Task 7 Decoding Formal Register

Translate this highly formal B2 university rule into simple, everyday English (like you are explaining it to a friend):

"Failure to vacate the dormitory premises by the stipulated deadline will result in the forfeiture of your security deposit."
Simple Translation: "If you don't leave your dorm room by the deadline, you will lose your deposit money."

(vacate = leave, premises = building/room, stipulated = agreed upon/stated, forfeiture = losing something as a penalty).

Task 8 Simulated Task: University Health Clinic

University Health Clinic

Patient Appointment & Cancellation Policy

1. Appointments and Walk Ins
The University Health Clinic operates primarily on an appointment basis to minimize wait times. While walk in patients are accepted for urgent medical concerns, they are subject to physician availability and may experience extended wait times. Students experiencing life threatening emergencies should bypass the clinic and call 911 immediately.

2. Late Arrival Policy
We ask that patients arrive 10 minutes prior to their scheduled appointment to complete necessary paperwork. A grace period of 15 minutes will be permitted for unforeseen delays. If a patient arrives more than 15 minutes late, their appointment will be automatically forfeited and marked as a "No Show."

3. Cancellation and "No Show" Fees
If you must cancel or reschedule an appointment, you are required to notify the clinic at least 24 hours in advance. Failure to do so, or failing to attend a scheduled appointment entirely, constitutes a "No Show." A $25 fee will be assessed to the student's university account for each No Show incident. This fee may be waived in cases of documented emergencies, subject to the clinic director's discretion.
