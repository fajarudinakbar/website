In your native language, if someone says, "It's freezing in here," they aren't usually asking for a weather report. They are making a polite request for you to close the window or turn up the heat. This is called a Speech Act, the hidden purpose behind the words.

To master the "Choose a Response" task, you cannot just translate words; you must translate the speaker's intent (e.g., Are they offering help? Apologizing? Asking for directions?).

The 3-Step "Response" Strategy

When reading or listening to short prompts, your brain has to react instantly. Follow these three rules:

1. Identify the Intent

Listen to the first word. Is it a "Wh " question requiring information? Is it a "Yes/No" question? Or is it a statement requiring sympathy or agreement?

2. Note the Register

Pay attention to the tone. A response to a professor ("I would be glad to help, Professor.") is different from a response to a roommate ("Sure thing!").

3. Avoid Sound Traps

Test makers use words that sound similar to trick you. Example prompt: "Where is the train?" Wrong choice: "It might rain today."

Target Prompt

Transcript: "Excuse me, Professor. I was wondering if you had a few minutes to discuss my essay draft? I'm really struggling with the conclusion."

Explanation:

The Speech Act is a polite request for help or time. Even though they say "I was wondering...", they are asking for an immediate meeting. A natural response from the professor would accept or decline the request politely, such as: "Sure, come into my office," or "I'm afraid I have a meeting now, can you come to office hours tomorrow?"
