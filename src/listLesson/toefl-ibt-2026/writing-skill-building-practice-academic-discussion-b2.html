"The use of Artificial Intelligence (AI) in classrooms will soon make traditional teachers unnecessary. Do you agree?"

Professor

As we discuss the future of education, some people argue that AI will eventually replace human teachers. They suggest that AI's ability to store and deliver information is superior to any human. What do you think? Should classrooms move toward AI-led instruction, or is the human teacher still essential?

Student 1 (Maya)

I believe human teachers are essential. Learning isn't just about receiving data; it’s about inspiration. A teacher can see when a student is frustrated or bored and adjust their tone. AI might have all the answers, but it doesn't have the empathy to truly "teach" a child.

M

Student 2 (Jordan)

I disagree with Maya. AI can be available 24/7 and doesn't get tired or biased. It can grade thousands of essays instantly and give every single student a personalized lesson plan. In a world where teachers are overworked, AI provides the efficiency that schools desperately need.

J
Sample High-Level Response
Model Answer

While I understand Jordan’s point regarding the efficiency and 24/7 availability of AI, I find myself agreeing more with Maya’s perspective. I believe that human teachers are irreplaceable because education is a social process, not just a transfer of information. In my own experience, the teachers who impacted me most were those who challenged my thinking and provided personalized mentorship during difficult projects.

Moreover, a human teacher is essential for developing critical thinking and soft skills, such as collaboration and ethics, which an algorithm cannot authentically model. AI can certainly be a powerful tool to assist with administrative tasks or data delivery, but it should remain a supplement to, rather than a replacement for, the human element in the classroom.

Tips for Success

  • Respond to Others: Acknowledge the points made by other students to show you are participating in a discussion.
  • State Your Position Clearly: Do not be neutral; choose a side and support it with your own reasoning or experiences.
  • Use Linking Words: Incorporate phrases like Moreover, On the other hand, or In addition to make your response cohesive.
  • Aim for Elaborated Ideas: An effective response is typically at least 100 words long and uses a variety of syntactic structures.
  • Check Your Mechanics: Ensure your response follows the conventions of English spelling and punctuation.
