Task 1 Warm Up Thinking

  • Why is it difficult for doctors to explain medical concepts to normal patients?
  • Have you ever had to explain a complex subject to someone who knew nothing about it? How did you do it?
  • Why do hospital ethics boards require research documents to be written in plain English?
Sample Ideas:
  • Doctors spend years learning complex vocabulary, so it becomes their natural way of speaking, making it hard to switch back to simple words.
  • Yes, I try to use everyday comparisons or metaphors to help them visualize the concept.
  • Ethics boards need plain English so that patients truly understand the risks before they agree to participate in a study.

Listening Transcript

Dr. Lee: "Good morning, Mr. Davis. Thank you for reviewing our research proposal. We are studying the EFFICACY of a new medication for patients with severe HYPERTENSION."

Mr. Davis: "Good morning, Dr. Lee. Could you explain that in plain English for the ethics board? We are not all medical doctors."

Dr. Lee: "Of course. We want to see how well this new drug lowers high blood pressure compared to a standard PLACEBO, which is a pill with no active medicine."

Mr. Davis: "I understand now. Are there any risks for the patients involved?"

Dr. Lee: "Yes, there is one major CONTRAINDICATION. Patients with a history of heart disease cannot take this drug because it might be dangerous for them."

Mr. Davis: "Thank you. How will you present the final results to the public?"

Dr. Lee: "We will SYNTHESIZE all the data into a simple report. We must ensure every participant gives fully informed CONSENT before we begin."

Mr. Davis: "Excellent. Clear communication is the key to ethical research."

Task 3 General Understanding

  • What is the setting and purpose of this conversation?
  • Who is Dr. Lee speaking to?
  • Why does Mr. Davis ask Dr. Lee to change the way she speaks?
Sample Answers:
  • Setting: A formal meeting reviewing a medical research proposal.
  • Audience: Mr. Davis, a member of the ethics board.
  • Reason: Because not everyone on the board is a medical doctor, so she needs to use plain English.

Task 4 Detail and Meaning

  • What specific medical condition is the new drug designed to treat?
  • What is being used to compare against the new drug in the study?
  • Which specific group of patients are not allowed to take this new drug?
Sample Answers:
  • Condition: Severe high blood pressure (hypertension).
  • Comparison: A standard placebo (a pill with no active medicine).
  • Excluded Group: Patients with a history of heart disease.

Task 9 Case Study Decision Making

Scenario:

A colleague is consistently late with their part of a shared research project. It is affecting your own work.

Option A: Use very soft language to ask if they need help, avoiding any mention of their lateness.
Option B: Use a "Sandwich" approach: Start with a compliment, clearly state the need for the data, and end with a polite request for a specific new deadline.

Discuss which option is more effective for a B1 professional.

Analysis:

Option B is more effective. The "Sandwich" approach balances professional accountability with politeness. Option A may protect the bond in the short term, but it fails to solve the work delay, which can lead to larger conflicts later. Option B uses structure to soften the direct request.
