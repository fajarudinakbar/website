Skill Focus: Identifying Arguments & Writing Integrated Responses

Level: B2 (Upper Intermediate)

Step 1: Understand the Task

In the TOEFL Integrated Writing task, you will:

  1. Read an academic passage (3 minutes).
  2. Listen to a lecture on the same topic (approx. 2 minutes).
  3. Write a response explaining how the lecture challenges the reading.

Important: Do not give your personal opinion. Focus only on comparing the ideas.

Step 2 & 3: Reading Passage & Comprehension

Replacing Printed Textbooks with Digital Textbooks

Many universities are considering replacing traditional printed textbooks with digital textbooks. Supporters of this change argue that digital textbooks offer several advantages over printed ones, especially in modern academic environments where technology is already widely used. According to the reading, the shift to digital textbooks would benefit students, institutions, and the environment.

One major advantage of digital textbooks is improved accessibility. Digital materials can be accessed on laptops, tablets, or smartphones, allowing students to study anytime and anywhere. In addition, digital textbooks often include features such as keyword searches, hyperlinks, and digital note-taking tools, which can help students study more efficiently.

Another benefit discussed in the article is cost reduction. Printed textbooks are often expensive, and new editions are released frequently, forcing students to buy updated versions. Digital textbooks are usually cheaper to produce and distribute, and universities may negotiate licenses that reduce costs for students.

Finally, the reading argues that digital textbooks are more environmentally friendly. Producing printed textbooks requires large amounts of paper, ink, and transportation. By switching to digital materials, universities could reduce paper use and lower their overall environmental impact.

Step 4 & 5: Lecture & Main Idea

Lecturer: While the reading suggests that replacing printed textbooks with digital ones would bring many benefits, these claims are not entirely convincing.

First, digital textbooks do not always improve accessibility. Not all students have reliable access to technology. Some lack personal devices, while others experience technical issues such as software errors or limited battery life. As a result, digital textbooks can make studying more difficult for certain students.

Second, the cost benefits of digital textbooks are unclear. Many digital textbooks are sold through subscription-based platforms that expire after a limited time. Once access ends, students cannot reuse or resell the materials. In contrast, printed textbooks can often be kept or sold, making them more cost-effective in the long term.

Finally, the environmental advantages of digital textbooks may be overstated. Producing electronic devices requires significant energy and raw materials, and electronic waste is a growing environmental problem. Increased screen use also leads to higher electricity consumption, reducing the environmental benefits of digital textbooks.

Explanation:

Model Response (approx. 185 words)

The reading and the lecture both examine the idea of replacing printed textbooks with digital textbooks at universities. While the reading supports this change by emphasizing accessibility, cost savings, and environmental benefits, the lecturer challenges each of these points.

First, the reading claims that digital textbooks improve accessibility because students can use them on multiple devices and study anywhere. In contrast, the lecturer explains that not all students have reliable access to technology, and technical problems can make digital materials difficult to use.

Second, the reading argues that digital textbooks reduce costs since they are cheaper to produce. However, the lecturer notes that many digital textbooks require subscriptions that expire, preventing students from reselling or reusing the materials.

Finally, the reading suggests that digital textbooks are environmentally friendly because they reduce paper use. The lecturer disputes this by pointing out that electronic devices require large amounts of energy and create electronic waste, which limits environmental benefits.

Why this essay works at B2 and for TOEFL:

  • Each body paragraph clearly contrasts reading vs. lecture
  • No personal opinion is included
