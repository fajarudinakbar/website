Task 2: Vocabulary in Context

Shopping Smart: Ethics and Trends during Ramadan

In Indonesia, the weeks before Ramadan and Idul Fitri are the busiest times for the economy. Recently, a new trend has changed how people buy clothes and food: the "Pre-Order" (PO) system. While this system helps small businesses manage their stock, it also creates new challenges for shoppers. Sellers often use psychological tactics to make people buy things quickly. This phenomenon is often driven by "FOMO," or the Fear of Missing Out, where customers feel they will lose a great opportunity if they do not pay immediately.

However, many experts argue that this high-pressure shopping culture goes against Islamic values. In Islamic ethics, or adab, shopping should be a calm and honest process. One of the most important principles is avoiding israf, which means excess or waste. When consumers are pressured by limited-time offers, they often buy items they do not really need. This creates a cycle of CONSUMERISM that can hurt a family's budget and distract them from the spiritual meaning of the holy month.

Another issue with the modern PO system is the lack of clarity in some transactions. In Islamic law, a business deal should be transparent. Both the buyer and the seller must understand the QUALITY, price, and delivery time of the product. Sometimes, the "Pre-Order" tactic involves long waiting times without clear information. This can lead to disappointment if the final product does not match the beautiful pictures seen online. To counter this, scholars suggest that buyers should practice MODERATION and patience.

For businesses, the challenge is to balance profit with ethics. Instead of using aggressive tactics to TRAP customers, sellers can adopt "Slow Shopping" ideas. This means giving customers enough time to think and providing honest descriptions of products. When a business is transparent, it builds a long-term relationship of trust with its TRANSACTIONS.

In conclusion, as the digital market grows, shoppers need to be more aware of the tactics used by brands. By following traditional values of moderation and honesty, Indonesians can enjoy the FESTIVE season without falling into the trap of unnecessary debt. Shopping should support the community and the family, not create stress. Understanding the difference between a real need and a manufactured "emergency" is the first step toward becoming a mindful consumer in the modern age.
