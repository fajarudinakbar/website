TOEFL iBT Reading: Question Types

Skill Focus: Understanding all TOEFL Reading question types and practicing each one.

Level: B1 (Intermediate)

1. Basic Information (Factual Detail)

What it tests: Information stated directly in the passage.

Strategy: Match the question to the exact sentence in the text.

Practice:

Cities are adding “micro-parks” to small empty spaces. These tiny parks may include a bench, a few trees, or a small garden. They give people a place to rest during a busy day.

What is one purpose of micro-parks?

Answer & Explanation

A. Micro-parks “give people a place to rest.” This is directly stated.

2. Vocabulary in Context

What it tests: Word meaning based on sentence context.

Strategy: Replace the word with a similar meaning that fits the sentence.

Practice:

The new science center is attracting visitors because of its “interactive” displays.

The word “interactive” is closest in meaning to:

Answer & Explanation

C. “Interactive” displays require visitors to touch or interact with them; “hands-on” matches this meaning.

3. Reference

What it tests: What a pronoun refers to in the text.

Strategy: Look one or two sentences before the pronoun.

Practice:

Students enjoy the school’s reading corner. It gives them a comfortable space to read after class.

The word “It” refers to:

Answer & Explanation

D. “It” refers to “the school’s reading corner,” the thing that gives students a comfortable space.

4. Sentence Simplification

What it tests: The essential meaning of a long sentence.

Strategy: Remove extra details; keep the core idea.

Practice:

Because the weather was changing quickly, the researchers decided to end the outdoor experiment earlier than planned.

Which option expresses the essential meaning?

Answer & Explanation

A. The essential idea: they ended the experiment early because the weather changed.

5. Inference

What it tests: Meaning that is suggested, not directly stated.

Strategy: Use evidence from the text, not personal opinion.

Practice:

The café added more tables outside, and now it is often crowded in the evening.

What can be inferred?

Answer & Explanation

A. If the café is often crowded after adding more tables, it suggests increased popularity.

6. Rhetorical Purpose

What it tests: Why the author includes a detail or example.

Strategy: Ask how the detail supports the main idea.

Practice:

The author mentions that the robot can climb stairs.

Why does the author include this detail?

Answer & Explanation

A. The detail about climbing stairs shows the robot can do useful actions.

7. Negative Factual Information

What it tests: Identifying what the passage does NOT say.

Strategy: Three options appear in the text; one does not.

Practice:

The city library offers free Wi-Fi, study rooms, and weekend workshops for students.

Which is NOT mentioned?

Answer & Explanation

D. The passage lists Wi-Fi, study rooms, and workshops—but not online tutoring.

8. Sentence Insertion

What it tests: How sentences connect logically within a paragraph.

Strategy: Match pronouns and ideas to the correct position.

Practice:

Insert the sentence:

“This idea encouraged many young people to start planting small gardens at home.”

Paragraph:

The school created a program that teaches students about growing vegetables. The lessons show how plants need water, sunlight, and good soil. Many students became excited about trying it themselves.

Where does the sentence fit?

Answer & Explanation

C. The sentence with "This idea" refers to the "program" and "lessons". The pronoun "it" in "trying it themselves" refers to "planting small gardens". The sentence fits just before "Many students became excited..." because it explains *why* they became excited.

9. Prose Summary (Reading-to-Learn)

What it tests: Choosing the two main ideas.

Strategy: Do not pick examples or small details.

Practice:

Local markets help communities in many ways. They support small farmers by giving them a place to sell their goods. They also make fresh food easier to access, especially for families who live far from supermarkets.

Which TWO sentences represent the most important ideas?

Answer & Explanation

A and C. Both express the passage’s main points: supporting farmers and helping families access fresh food. (B is not mentioned, D is a small detail).

10. Fill-in-a-Table (Reading-to-Learn)

What it tests: Organizing information into categories.

Strategy: Identify which details belong to each side.

Practice:

Solar panels and wind turbines are two common clean-energy technologies. Solar panels work best in sunny areas, while wind turbines are effective in places with strong winds. Both help reduce pollution by generating power without burning fuel.

Categorize the options:

A. Work well in sunny areas
B. Work well in windy areas
C. Create pollution when running
D. Generate energy without burning fuel

Answer & Explanation

  • Solar Panels → A (Passage says "Solar panels work best in sunny areas")
  • Wind Turbines → B (Passage says "wind turbines are effective in places with strong winds")
  • Both technologies → D (Passage says "Both help reduce pollution by generating power without burning fuel")
  • The detail about pollution (C) is Incorrect because both generate clean energy.
