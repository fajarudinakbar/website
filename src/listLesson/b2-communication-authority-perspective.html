Global Design, Local Impact

A global L&D initiative is designed at headquarters and rolled out across multiple regions. The programme aims to standardise leadership behaviours and accelerate performance. While global leaders are confident in the design, several regional teams report low engagement and limited relevance to their local contexts.

Regional feedback indicates that examples, scenarios, and expectations do not reflect cultural norms, operational realities, or maturity levels in certain markets. Local L&D teams feel the programme was imposed rather than co-created. However, global stakeholders are concerned that too much localisation may dilute consistency and weaken the global leadership brand.

You are asked to share your perspective during a global review meeting. Your responsibility is to evaluate the situation objectively, represent regional realities, and communicate implications clearly without positioning the issue as resistance or failure.

3. Critical Discussion

Why do regional teams perceive the programme as low in relevance?

What concerns do global stakeholders have about localisation?

Why is it important to separate feedback from criticism in this situation?

Sample Responses:

  • Relevance: The programme's examples and scenarios do not reflect local cultural norms, operational realities, or market maturity.
  • Global Concerns: Stakeholders worry that adapting the content too much will dilute consistency and weaken the unified global brand.
  • Feedback vs Criticism: Criticism can sound like resistance or complaining; feedback focuses on objective data regarding impact and engagement, which is professional and constructive.

4.2 Speaking Focus – Stating Authority

How can you express authority when your perspective differs from global leadership’s assumptions?

5.1 Evaluating the Situation

Scenario: Global leaders believe the problem is poor communication, not programme design.
How would you evaluate this claim professionally? What evidence would you highlight?

Evaluation: Distinguish between understanding (communication) and applicability (design).
Evidence: "We can see that the communication channels were effective (high open rates), but the feedback specifically cites irrelevant scenarios (content)."
Tone: Data-driven and neutral, avoiding blame on any specific team.

5.2 Explaining Stakeholder Impact

Scenario: You must explain the situation to headquarters. How does the programme affect regional managers and local L&D teams? Why should global leaders care?

Impact: Regional managers feel disconnected; Local L&D teams struggle to "sell" a product they don't believe in.
Why Care: "If regional teams disengage, the global leadership brand weakens, and the investment yields zero return in these markets."

5.3 Responding to Alternative Viewpoints

Scenario: A global leader says, “Local teams always resist change.”
Why is this framing problematic? How could you reframe it constructively?

Problem: It dismisses legitimate operational concerns as emotional resistance.
Reframing: “Rather than resistance to change, the data suggests they are highlighting specific contextual factors that prevent them from applying these tools effectively.”
