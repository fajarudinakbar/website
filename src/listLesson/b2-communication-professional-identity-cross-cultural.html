Speaking Up Across Cultures and Hierarchy

You are participating in a regional meeting attended by managers and C-level leaders from several countries. Communication styles vary widely. Some participants speak very directly, while others use indirect language to show respect. English is the working language, but it is not the first language for most participants.

During the meeting, a strategic direction is proposed that you believe will create long-term problems. From your professional perspective, the plan overlooks operational risks and cultural readiness in several regions. However, you hesitate to speak. You are concerned about appearing disrespectful, losing face, or challenging senior authority in a public setting.

As a professional, you are expected to contribute judgment, not silence. The challenge is to express disagreement clearly, frame it from your role, and remain culturally appropriate. This requires separating respect from hesitation and clarity from confrontation.

3. Critical Discussion

Why does the meeting context increase pressure to remain silent?

What professional risks arise if you choose not to voice your concerns?

How does the reading distinguish between respect and hesitation?

Sample Responses:

  • Context Pressure: The presence of senior C-level leaders and varying cultural norms about hierarchy discourages open disagreement.
  • Professional Risks: Silence leads to unshared risks, potential strategic failure, and poor decision-making by the leadership.
  • Respect vs Hesitation: Respect is treating others with dignity; hesitation is holding back necessary professional judgment due to fear.

5.1 Disagreeing with C-Level Stakeholders

Scenario: A C-level executive proposes a strategy you believe is risky.
How should you frame your disagreement? What phrases signal respect without weakening clarity?

Strategy: Connect your disagreement to your specific role (e.g., "From an operational perspective...").
Phrases: "I see the value in this strategy; however, I must flag a potential risk regarding..."
Tone: Objective, calm, and focused on company success rather than personal opinion.

5.2 Reformulating Overly Polite Language

Original: “I might be wrong, but maybe we could think about another option.”
Why does this reduce impact? How can it be reformulated?

Critique: "I might be wrong" destroys credibility before the idea is even heard.
Reformulated: “From my perspective, there may be operational risks with this approach, and I would recommend considering an alternative.”

5.3 Impromptu Professional Speaking

Scenario: You are suddenly asked, “Do you support this direction?”
How can you buy time? What structure helps you answer clearly?

Buying Time: "That is a crucial question. Let me consider the implications for a moment."
Structure: PREP (Point, Reason, Example, Point) or "Yes/No, because [Reason], therefore [Conclusion]."
