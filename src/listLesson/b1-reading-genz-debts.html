The Digital Money Trap: Why Gen Z Is at Risk

In today’s digital world, money products are everywhere. People can use paylater services, online loans (pinjol), or investment apps with just a few clicks. At first, this looks positive because access is easy. But for many young people, especially Generation Z (Gen Z), these products can be dangerous. They often do not understand the hidden COSTS, and this can lead to serious debt problems.

Debt Feels Normal

Many Gen Z learn about money from social media. They see influencers talking about “financial freedom” or showing tricks to get rich quickly. At the same time, other posts promote paylater and pinjol as smart ways to manage cash. As a result, borrowing money for shopping becomes NORMAL. Some young people even use loans to buy luxury items, without thinking about the interest and extra fees they must pay later.

Smart Promotions

Banks and fintech companies use strong marketing to ATTRACT young customers. They design colorful ads, use friendly language, and even make promotions look like games. For example, a video on TikTok may look like a pizza discount tip, but in reality, it is an ad for a loan app. These promotions rarely explain the risks. Because of this, many young people fall into debt traps.

Social Media Influence

There are also positive efforts. Some influencers and educators share content about how to manage money wisely. For example, platforms like RencanaKu.ID provide free lessons on financial planning. However, these educational posts are less POPULAR than flashy content that promises quick wealth. This shows that financial literacy in Indonesia is still low compared to the wide access to financial products.

What Can Be Done?

The problem is not only Gen Z’s fault. Companies and the government also play a role. Young people should learn to read terms and conditions carefully before using financial services. They must remember that a “discount” with paylater is still debt. If they keep buying things with loans, stress will follow. At the same time, the government and financial authorities need stronger rules to control marketing and protect consumers. Banks and fintech companies should be more TRANSPARENT about costs. Communities and schools can also help by spreading accurate financial information.

In the end, managing money wisely requires cooperation. Gen Z, educators, companies, and the government must work together. Only then can young people avoid the SWEET promises of digital finance and build a stable future.
