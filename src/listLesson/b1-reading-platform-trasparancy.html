Big Tech Platforms Fail Transparency Test

JAKARTA - A recent international study has revealed that many of the world's largest digital platforms are failing to meet basic standards for advertising transparency. The report, titled "Data Not Found," suggests that apps like TikTok, Instagram, and YouTube are not providing enough information about how they target users with digital ads.

A Lack of Openness

Researchers from NetLab in Brazil and the University of Cambridge in the UK examined 15 major platforms. They found that half of these companies do not follow minimum rules for being open with the public. Transparency in this context means being clear about who pays for an advertisement, how much it costs, and which groups of people are being targeted.

The study highlighted several major problems. Many platforms have "ad libraries" meant to show active commercials, but these lists are often incomplete. Furthermore, it is very difficult for independent experts to access the data they need to verify if these companies are following local laws.

Different Standards Worldwide

The research compared how these platforms behave in three different regions:

  • The European Union (EU): This region has the world's strongest rules, known as the Digital Services Act. However, even with these laws, platforms still find ways to limit the data they share.
  • The United Kingdom (UK): The UK does not have a single law for everyone. Instead, the government checks companies one by one, which creates a lack of consistency.
  • Brazil: Representing the "Global South," Brazil has very few regulations. In this region, transparency is much lower because users must rely on the "goodwill" of the tech companies.

Risks to the Public

When companies are not honest about their advertising data, it creates serious risks for society. Experts warn that a lack of transparency allows disinformation, or fake news, to spread more easily. Some ads may look like normal posts but are actually designed to trick people.

There are also concerns regarding safety. Without proper data, it is hard to stop scams that target vulnerable people or prevent inappropriate content from reaching children and teenagers.

The Future of Digital Rules

Currently, digital platforms collect a massive amount of data about their users, but the users know very little about how the platforms operate. The authors of the study argue that transparency should be a legal requirement rather than a choice. They believe that for the internet to be safe, governments must force these companies to be more open with their data.

As the debate continues, researchers and world leaders are calling for new international standards to ensure that "Big Tech" remains accountable to the people who use their services every day.
