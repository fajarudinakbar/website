Green Fiscal Policy and the Environment

Climate change and environmental damage are serious problems today. The World Meteorological Organization reported that the years 2015–2024 were the hottest decade since 1850. In Indonesia, major environmental disasters happened at the end of 2025. Floods and landslides affected Aceh, North Sumatra, and West Sumatra. According to the National Disaster Management Agency, many people died, went missing, or were injured.

These disasters did not happen suddenly. They were caused by long-term environmental damage. Problems such as deforestation, illegal mining, poor waste management, and land conversion have damaged nature for many years. These activities make floods and landslides more likely. Because of this, action is needed to protect the environment and prevent future disasters.

One solution is Green Fiscal Policy. This policy uses taxes and government income to protect nature. It is based on the idea of “Polluter Pays,” which means that people or companies who cause pollution must pay for the damage they create. This idea was first used in developed countries and is now discussed in Indonesia.

Indonesia already has some environmental rules. For example, there is a carbon tax and special funds for forestry. However, many experts believe these rules are not strong enough. Environmental damage still continues, and there is a gap between green promises and real actions.

To work better, Green Fiscal Policy needs improvement. Taxes should cover more activities such as forest use, plastic waste, and land conversion. The government should also support green technology by giving tax cuts or subsidies for clean energy, electric vehicles, and recycling tools. Money from environmental taxes should be used to repair nature, improve public transport, and support renewable energy research.

Green Fiscal Policy can also help people affected by disasters. Funds can support victims of floods and landslides and help rebuild damaged areas. Most importantly, this policy encourages people to think more carefully about their actions and their impact on the environment.

In conclusion, Green Fiscal Policy is not only about collecting money. It is a way to guide people toward protecting nature. With cooperation between the government and society, Indonesia can build a sustainable, greener, and safer future.
