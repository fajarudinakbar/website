In the "Multiple Choice, Multiple Answer" task, what happens if you select one correct option and one incorrect option?

Mastering the High-Point Tasks

While Multiple Choice questions are familiar, the Fill in the Blanks (Reading & Writing) and Re-order Paragraphs tasks contribute significantly more to your score. Success here depends on identifying patterns.

1. Academic Collocations (The Secret Weapon)

Collocations are words that "live together." Native speakers know them instinctively, but B2 learners must memorize them. In PTE "Fill in the Blanks," looking at the word immediately before or after the gap often gives the answer without reading the whole text.

  • Verb + Noun: pose a risk, make a decision, fulfill a requirement.
  • Adjective + Noun: academic performance, alternative energy, brief overview.
  • Prepositional Phrases: associated with, rely on, consistent with.

2. Text Cohesion (For Re-ordering)

When re-ordering paragraphs, look for "glue" words that link ideas. A sentence is rarely independent.

  • Definite Articles (The): "A student..." usually comes before "The student...".
  • Pronouns (This/That/It/They): "This theory..." must follow a sentence explaining the theory.
  • Transition Signals: However, Therefore, Consequently, For example.

Task 7 Re-order Logic Challenge

Identify the correct order of these three sentences:

A. However, this method proved to be unreliable in humid conditions.

B. Early scientists attempted to measure time using water clocks.

C. Consequently, they began searching for mechanical alternatives.

Correct Order: B - A - C
1. (B) "Early scientists..." is the topic sentence introducing the subject.
2. (A) "However, this method..." refers back to "water clocks" and introduces a problem.
3. (C) "Consequently..." is the result of the problem (unreliability).
