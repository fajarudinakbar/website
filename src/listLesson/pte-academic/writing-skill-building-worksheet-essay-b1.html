Instructions: You have 20 minutes to plan, write, and revise a 200-300 word essay. At the B1 level, your focus is on answering the prompt directly, maintaining a logical structure, and avoiding generic memorized templates.

Step 1 Contextual Warm-up: The "Zero Content" Trap

Many students write beautifully structured 250-word essays but receive a total score of 0.

Predict: Based on the PTE rules, what is the single biggest mistake that results in an automatic 0 for the entire essay?

The Rule: If the essay is written on a completely different topic from the prompt (Content = 0), you receive NO FURTHER SCORING. Form, Grammar, and Vocabulary are not even checked. Always answer the prompt directly!

Step 3 Strategy Spotlight: The Word Count Rule

The 'Form' score is based strictly on word count. You get 2 points for 200-300 words.
Action: What score do you get if you write 195 words, and what score do you get if you write 115 words?

Strategy: 195 words gets a 1 (partial credit applies to 120-199 and 301-380 words). 115 words gets a 0 (less than 120 words or more than 380 words). Aim strictly for 220-280 words to be safe.

Step 4 Analyzing a Flawed Essay

Read the prompt and the B1-level student's body paragraph. Do not memorize theory; spot the errors.

Prompt: "Some people believe that universities should be free for everyone. Others think students should pay. Discuss both views."

Student's Body Paragraph:

"Many people think that universities should be free for everyone. This is a very interesting topic in modern society. Universities should be free for everyone because education is good. Also, some people believe that universities should be free for everyone to get a job. In my opinion, universities should be free for everyone."

Step 5 Rapid Concept Check

Answer these 3 questions based on the PTE writing criteria.

1. The student uses the exact phrase "universities should be free for everyone" 4 times. What PTE score trait does this ruin?

2. "This is a very interesting topic in modern society." Why does PTE AI penalize this sentence?

3. Did the student "Discuss both views" as requested by the prompt?

Answers:
1. Vocabulary Range / Content. Repeating prompt language shows a lack of vocabulary and reformulating skills.
2. It is a generic, memorized statement. The PTE AI detects "memorized material" and scores it as superficial or irrelevant.
3. No. They only discussed the "free" view, leading to a lower Content score (partial addressing of the prompt).

You must know the exact rules of PTE Write Essay to score efficiently. Select the correct options.

Step 7 Fill-in-the-Blanks: Cohesive Devices

Use connective devices to link your sentences (Development, Structure, and Coherence trait).

, free university tuition removes financial barriers for low-income families. , more students can access higher education and improve their job prospects. , critics argue that this policy would place a massive burden on taxpayers.

Step 8 Paragraph Re-order Drill

A high-scoring B1 essay has a logical structure. Arrange these sentences to form a coherent Body Paragraph.

A. For example, a student from a poor background could become a doctor if tuition fees were removed.

B. Firstly, making education free ensures equal opportunities for everyone.

C. As a result, society benefits from a more skilled and diverse workforce.

D. This means that wealth no longer dictates a person's future career path.

Correct Sequence: B - D - A - C
1. Topic Sentence (B)
2. Explanation (D - "This means...")
3. Example (A - "For example...")
4. Concluding thought/Result (C - "As a result...")

Step 9 Scenario Application: Paraphrasing

To avoid the "0 Content" trap, you must paraphrase the prompt.
Prompt: "Some people believe that universities should be free for everyone."
Task: Rewrite this statement using your own words.

Example Paraphrases:
- "It is argued by some that higher education should be provided at no cost to all individuals."
- "A common belief is that the government should fully subsidize tertiary education."
(Notice how "universities" became "higher/tertiary education" and "free" became "at no cost/subsidize".)
