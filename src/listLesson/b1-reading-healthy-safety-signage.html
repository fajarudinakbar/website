Municipal Guide: Shinjuku Medical District Safety and Operations

Welcome to the Shinjuku Medical District. For your safety and professional coordination, please review these CRITICAL instructions regarding our facilities and emergency protocols.

Section 1

Clinic Operations

The General Health Clinic is open for walk in patients from Monday to Friday, 09:00 to 18:00. Please note there is a lunch closure between 12:30 and 14:30. Saturday hours are restricted, running from 09:00 until 13:00.

The clinic is closed on Sundays and Public Holidays. Emergency medical services are available 24 hours a day at the DESIGNATED Regional Hospital located two blocks away.

Section 2

Emergency Evacuation

In the event of an earthquake or fire, please remain calm. Follow the green directional arrows to reach the nearest ASSEMBLY point located in the Central Park. Do not use elevators under any circumstances; use the emergency stairs.

The EVACUATION route is clearly marked on every floor of the medical center. Please stay in the safe zone for the DURATION of the emergency alert.

Section 3

Community Safety

Smoking and the use of electronic cigarettes are strictly PROHIBITED within the clinic premises and surrounding gardens.

To ensure COMPLIANCE with local fire safety regulations, please keep all corridors clear of personal belongings. In case of a medical emergency in the street, use the yellow emergency phones located at every intersection.

Task 9: Case Study Decision Making

Scenario:

You are treating a patient at 12:25 PM. The clinic is supposed to close for lunch at 12:30 PM. The patient has a minor issue but wants to talk for a long time.

  • Option A: Politely interrupt the patient and explain the clinic's mandatory lunch closure and ask them to return at 14:30.
  • Option B: Continue the session through the lunch break to ensure the patient feels heard, even though you will miss your break and the clinic doors will be locked.

Discuss the professional consequences of both choices.

Analysis: Option A reinforces professional boundaries and adheres to clinic operational rules, which protects staff well being. However, it requires strong communication skills to avoid offending the patient. Option B builds immediate rapport but violates clinic policy, risks burnout, and sets a precedent that rules can be broken, which can disrupt the entire clinic schedule. Option A is the more sustainable professional choice.
