Gen Z Struggles to Stop Scrolling: Experts Explain Why

For Generation Z, social media has become a daily HABIT. From TikTok to Instagram, young people spend hours scrolling through photos and videos. While this connects them with friends and trends, many find it hard to put their phones down. Experts say this is not accidental; apps are DESIGNED to keep users’ attention for as long as possible.

The main reason scrolling feels so good is DOPAMINE. This is a chemical in the brain that creates pleasure. Every time a user sees a funny video or gets a "like," the brain releases dopamine. This makes the user want to stay on the app to get more of that good feeling. Over time, this cycle can become ADDICTIVE, making it difficult to stop even when they want to.

However, too much scrolling has negative effects. Many young people report feeling tired, anxious, or unable to focus on schoolwork. Seeing perfect lives online can also make some users feel INSECURE about their own lives. They compare themselves to influencers and feel they are not good enough.

Despite these risks, social media also has benefits. It allows Gen Z to learn new things, find communities, and express themselves creatively. The goal, experts suggest, is not to delete all apps but to find BALANCE. By setting time limits and being mindful of how they feel, young people can enjoy social media without letting it control their lives.

5.1 Students and Scrolling

Scenario: Student tired/unfocused due to scrolling. Why? Change what? Benefit?

Why: Late night scrolling disrupts sleep and dopamine cycles affect focus.
Change: No phone in bedroom or set a strict "bedtime" for devices.
Benefit: Better sleep leads to better concentration at school.

5.2 Social Media for Learning

Scenario: Using social media for education. Useful content? Risks? Staying focused?

Useful: Educational videos, language tips, science experiments.
Risk: Getting distracted by entertainment or comments.
Focus: Use specific hashtags or lists, set a timer for research.

5.3 Digital Detox

Scenario: School plans detox day. Helpful? Problems? Realistic?

Helpful: Resets attention span and encourages face-to-face talk.
Problems: Students might feel anxious (FOMO) or bored.
Realistic: Yes, if planned with fun alternative activities (sports, games).
