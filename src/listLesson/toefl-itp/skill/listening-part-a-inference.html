Task 1 "Kode" vs. Inference

In Indonesia, we often use "basa-basi" or "kode" (indirect codes) instead of saying "No" directly. English speakers do this too!

Example: If you ask a friend to eat, and they say "I just had a big lunch," what do they actually mean?

Meaning: They are refusing your invitation (No, thanks).

TOEFL Tip: In Part A, if the second speaker gives a reason/excuse, the answer is usually a Refusal.

Task 3 The "Sound Trap" Rule

If you hear the word "department" in the audio, and answer (A) says "apartment", should you choose it? Why or why not?

The Rule: Avoid similar sounds! If an answer choice contains a word that sounds exactly like a word in the conversation, it is usually a distractor (wrong answer). Focus on the meaning, not the sound.

Strategies for Short Conversations

TOEFL ITP Listening Part A consists of 30 short conversations. Each conversation is followed by a single question. The most critical strategy for this section is to focus on the second speaker. While the first speaker provides the context, the second speaker usually provides the answer.

1. Suggestions

The second speaker often suggests a solution. Look for phrases like "Why not...", "Let's...", or "If I were you...". The correct answer will often use a synonym, such as "recommend," "should," or "advise."

2. Polite Refusals

When invited to do something, speakers rarely say "No" directly. Instead, they offer an excuse.

Example: "I'd love to, but I have a paper due."
Meaning: He cannot go.

If the second speaker expresses uncertainty ("I'm not sure if..."), the answer usually implies they probably will not do it.

3. Agreement

If the second speaker says "You can say that again" or "I couldn't agree more," they are agreeing emphatically. The answer choice will usually say "She shares the man's opinion."

4. Passive Statements

If the dialogue uses the active voice ("The committee approved the budget"), the correct answer often uses the passive voice ("The budget was accepted"). This tests your ability to understand structure changes while keeping the same meaning.

Task 6 Strategy True or False

Task 7 Identify the Function

Read this line: "If I were you, I would speak to the professor immediately."

What is the function of this sentence? (e.g., Agreement, Refusal, Suggestion, Surprise)

Function: Suggestion (or Advice).

Common answers for this include: "She recommends speaking to the instructor" or "He should contact the professor."

Task 8 Analyzing Refusals

Why do speakers in TOEFL (and real life) rarely say "No" directly? Why do they give excuses instead?

Analysis: It is considered polite (social etiquette) to soften a refusal with a reason. Direct "No" can sound rude or aggressive.
