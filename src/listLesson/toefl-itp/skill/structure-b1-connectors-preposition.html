Topic: Connectors vs. Prepositions (Clauses vs. Phrases)

Strategy Guide

Instructions: One of the most common traps in the Structure section is confusing words that join clauses (Connectors) with words that join nouns (Prepositions). Complete the 10 steps below using the dropdown menus and radio buttons to build your accuracy.

Step 1 Skill Diagnostic

Decide if the sentence requires a Connector (because it is followed by a full subject+verb clause) or a Preposition (because it is followed only by a noun phrase).

1. _______ the heavy rain, the ferry to Bali was delayed.
2. The student received a scholarship _______ she had excellent grades.
3. _______ 2024, the new toll road was officially opened.
Diagnostic Key:
  • 1. Preposition: "the heavy rain" is a noun phrase. (e.g., Because of)
  • 2. Connector: "she had excellent grades" is a full Subject-Verb clause. (e.g., because)
  • 3. Preposition: "2024" is a noun indicating time. (e.g., In)

Step 3 Pattern Identification

Identify the specific grammatical trap in this sentence: "Because the heavy traffic in Jakarta, we missed our morning flight."

Trap Identified: Connector vs. Preposition Confusion.

"Because" is a connector that must be followed by a Subject + Verb. However, "the heavy traffic in Jakarta" is just a noun phrase. The sentence should use the preposition "Because of" or "Due to".

The Trans-Java Toll Road: Connecting an Island

Stretching across the island of Java, the Trans-Java Toll Road is a massive infrastructure network. Because of the region's dense population and rapid economic growth, the government prioritized this development. In 2015, major sections of the road were expedited to facilitate smoother logistics and passenger travel.

Although the project faced significant delays during its initial phases, construction eventually accelerated. Because planners successfully acquired the necessary land, they were able to connect Jakarta to Surabaya efficiently. Despite the high costs associated with the construction, economists agree that the long-term benefits for local businesses are invaluable.

Step 8 Structural Transformation

Choose the sentence that correctly transforms the clause (using Although) into a phrase (using Despite) without changing the meaning.

"Although the weather was bad, the ferry departed for Lombok on time."

Explanation:

The correct sentence is:
"Despite the bad weather, the ferry departed for Lombok on time."

Rule: "Despite" is a preposition. It must be followed by a noun phrase ("the bad weather"). "Despite" never takes the word "of" (unlike "in spite of").

Step 9 The "Wrong-Option" Analysis

In TOEFL ITP, eliminating traps is key. Look at this sentence missing its first word:

_______ the library was closed for renovations, the students went to a cafe to study.

(A) Due to

(B) Because

(C) Despite

(D) Therefore

The correct answer is (B) Because, since it connects two clauses. Your task is to identify why A, C, and D are traps.

(A)
(C)
(D)
Expert Breakdown:
  • (A) Due to: Wrong because it is a preposition. It must be followed by a noun phrase, not the clause "the library was closed".
  • (C) Despite: Wrong structurally (it is a preposition) AND logically (the meaning requires a cause-and-effect relationship, not contrast).
  • (D) Therefore: Wrong because transition words like "Therefore" or "Thus" cannot link two dependent clauses in this comma-separated structure without a semicolon or period.
