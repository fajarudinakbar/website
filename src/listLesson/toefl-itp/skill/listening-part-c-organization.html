Following the Structure

Topic: Signal Words and The 3-Point Note Strategy

Strategy Guide

Instructions: Part C involves academic talks (Lectures). Do not try to write everything. Focus on the Structure using "Signal Words" (First, Next, However) to identify the Main Idea and 2 Key Points.

Task 1 The GPS Analogy

When you use a navigation app, it tells you "Turn Left in 200 meters." In a lecture, the professor also gives you "audio signs" to tell you where the talk is going.

Question: Why do professors use words like "However" or "For instance"?

Answer: These are Signal Words.
  • "However" signals a change or contrast (Turn around!).
  • "For instance" signals an example (Look at this detail!).

If you hear these, pay close attention!

Task 2 Matching Signal Words

Match the signal word to its function (what it tells you is coming next).

Task 3 The "Minimalist Note" Strategy

You cannot write everything. For Part C, try to write only 3 things. What are they?

The Rule of 3:
  1. The Topic: What is the lecture about? (Intro)
  2. Key Point 1: The first major example or reason.
  3. Key Point 2: The second major example or reason.

Do not write full sentences. Use keywords only!

How Lectures are Structured

Academic lectures in TOEFL Part C are organized exactly like a formal essay. They do not wander randomly; they follow a strict structure. If you know the structure, you can predict the questions.

1. The Introduction (The "Hook" and Thesis)

The professor will usually start by connecting to a previous class ("Last week, we discussed...") or stating the goal clearly ("Today, I want to focus on...").

Strategy: The Main Idea question usually comes from the first 2-3 sentences.

2. The Body (Points and Examples)

This is where the details are. Professors use Transition Words (Signal Words) to separate ideas.

  • Chronology: First, Next, Subsequently, Finally.
  • Illustration: For example, To illustrate, Consider the case of...
  • Contrast: On the other hand, Conversely, However, Unlike...

3. The Conclusion

The talk often ends with a summary or a look at the future ("So, we can see that...", "In the next class, we will look at...").

Task 4 Signal Word Rapid Fire

Classify these words: Therefore, In addition, Consequently, On the contrary.

  • Addition: In addition
  • Result: Therefore, Consequently
  • Contrast: On the contrary

Task 7 3-Point Note Taking

Script: "Today, we will look at animal adaptation in deserts. The most common adaptation is 'avoidance'. Many animals, like the kangaroo rat, simply avoid the heat by staying underground during the day. Another method is 'dissipation'. Jackrabbits, for instance, have huge ears that help release body heat into the air."
  • Topic: Animal Adaptation (Desert)
  • Point 1: Avoidance (Stay underground / Kangaroo rat)
  • Point 2: Dissipation (Release heat / Big ears / Jackrabbit)

Task 8 The Purpose of Examples

Why do professors give specific examples (like "kangaroo rats" or "steam engines") after stating a main point? Why not just state the point?

Analysis: Examples clarify complex abstract ideas. In TOEFL, usually one question asks about the Main Idea, and another asks about the Specific Example used to support it.
