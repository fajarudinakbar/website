Navigating Part B

TOEFL ITP Listening Part B consists of 2 longer conversations (60-90 seconds) between two people. Unlike Part A, these conversations are not just one or two lines. They involve a back-and-forth discussion about a specific topic.

1. The Structure of the Conversation

Most TOEFL Part B conversations follow a predictable pattern:

  • Greeting and Problem Statement: The first speaker usually states why they are there (e.g., "Professor, I wanted to ask about the assignment...").
  • Discussion/Details: The second speaker asks questions or gives details.
  • Resolution/Closing: They decide on a plan of action.

2. The "Gist" Questions

The first question you are asked is almost always a "Gist" or "Main Idea" question. These questions sound like:

  • "What are the speakers mainly discussing?"
  • "Why does the student go to see the professor?"
  • "What is the conversation mainly about?"

3. Campus vs. Academic

Topics usually fall into two categories:

Campus Life: Housing, cafeteria food, library fines, clubs.

Academic: Discussing a research paper, missing an exam, clarifying a lecture topic.

Task 4 Strategy Check

If the conversation starts with "I am having trouble with my roommate", what is likely the main topic?

Answer: A housing or dormitory problem (Campus Life). The purpose is likely seeking advice on how to handle the roommate.

Task 8 Topic vs. Purpose

Sometimes the Topic is "Grades", but the Purpose is "To ask for an extension." Why is it important to know the difference?

Explanation: The topic is what they are talking about (the subject matter). The purpose is why the conversation is happening (the goal). TOEFL questions often ask "Why does the man visit the woman?" which is a Purpose question.

Task 10 Analyzing Structure

Professor Sure, come in. What seems to be the trouble?

Student I cannot find enough sources on my topic, 'The History of Kites'.

Professor Well, that is very specific. Why do you not broaden it to 'Early Aviation'?

Identify the Situation, the Problem, and the Solution.

Analysis:
  • Situation: Student visiting Professor (Office Hours).
  • Problem: Lack of sources for the research paper.
  • Solution: Broaden the topic to "Early Aviation."
