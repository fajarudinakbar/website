Step 3 Pattern Identification

Identify the specific "ITP Trap" in the following sentence: "The researcher working in the laboratory in Bandung."

Trap Identified: Missing Verb / Fake Verb Trap.

The word "working" is a present participle functioning as an adjective, not a main verb. A correct version would be: "The researcher is working in the laboratory."

Step 8 Structural Transformation

Step 9 The "Wrong-Option" Analysis

In TOEFL ITP, knowing why an option is wrong is just as important as finding the correct one. Look at this question:

_______ , the capital of Indonesia, faces severe challenges with land subsidence.

(A) Jakarta

(B) It is Jakarta

(C) Jakarta is

(D) That Jakarta

The correct answer is (A). But your task is to label why B, C, and D are traps.

(B)
(C)
(D)
Expert Breakdown:
  • (B) It is Jakarta: This creates a "Double Subject / Extra Verb" trap. The sentence already has a main verb ("faces"). Inserting "It is" creates a clause run-on without a connector.
  • (C) Jakarta is: This also creates an "Extra Verb" trap. It gives the sentence two verbs ("is" and "faces") but no connector to join the clauses.
  • (D) That Jakarta: This turns the subject into a "Noun Clause", which makes the structure incomplete. Noun clauses starting with "That" need their own internal verb, which is missing here.

Speed Drill Answer Key:
  • 1. (A) As "As" acts as a preposition here creating a prepositional phrase. (B) creates a double verb trap.
  • 2. (B) are preparing The relative pronoun "who" needs a full verb inside its clause. "Preparing" alone is incomplete.
  • 3. (C) declaring This is a reduced adjective clause (active voice) acting as an appositive modifier. (B) is also grammatically possible, but "declaring" is the standard participial phrase tested here. Both B and C are accepted as learning points.
  • 4. (B) did Indonesia implement Negative inversion rule. When a sentence starts with "Not until", the subject and auxiliary verb are inverted.
  • 5. (B) Becoming a medical doctor A gerund phrase acting as the singular subject of the main verb "requires".
