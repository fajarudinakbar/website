Indonesian Rupiah Hits Record Low Against US Dollar: A Look at the History

JAKARTA – On Monday, January 19, 2026, the Indonesian currency, the rupiah, reached its lowest value in history. The rupiah closed at 16,935 against the US dollar. This new record marks a difficult time for the nation’s economy. Financial experts say this is part of a negative trend that began last year.

The current situation is caused by several factors. First, the US dollar is very strong because the American economy is performing better than expected. Second, investors are worried about Indonesia’s financial health. In 2025, the government’s budget deficit reached 2.92%, which means the country spent more money than it earned.

To understand this event, it is helpful to look back at the history of the rupiah. There have been ten "dark points" where the currency faced extreme pressure.

The Early Crises (1997–2001)

The first major crisis happened in late 1997. The rupiah lost value so quickly that the government had to ask for help from the International Monetary Fund (IMF). Shortly after, in 1998, the situation became even worse. Economic problems turned into political and social unrest, leading to the resignation of President Soeharto. During this time, the rupiah crashed to 16,800. In 2001, political uncertainty returned during the presidency of Abdurrahman Wahid, causing another drop in value.

Global Challenges (2008–2018)

In 2008, a global financial crisis began in the United States. Many investors around the world became afraid and moved their money into safe assets like the US dollar. As a result, the rupiah fell significantly. In 2015, the currency faced new pressure when China changed its trade policies and the US central bank raised interest rates. Later, in 2018, economic problems in countries like Turkey and Argentina caused investors to worry about all developing markets, including Indonesia.

The Pandemic and Modern Struggles (2020–2026)

In March 2020, the COVID-19 pandemic caused global panic. Investors sold their Indonesian stocks and bonds quickly. This caused the rupiah to fall below 16,000 for the first time in over twenty years. More recently, the rupiah has struggled due to high interest rates in the US throughout 2024. In April 2025, new trade tariffs announced by the US government made the situation even more difficult for international trade. Finally, this week, the currency hit its newest low point of 16,935.

Why This Matters

When the rupiah is weak, it becomes more expensive for Indonesia to buy goods from other countries, such as oil, electronics, and food. This often leads to higher prices for everyday items, which is known as inflation. However, a weak currency can also make Indonesian products cheaper for people in other countries to buy, which might help Indonesian exporters.

The Indonesian government and the central bank are watching the situation closely. They want to make sure the economy remains stable. While the current numbers are a concern, history shows that the rupiah has recovered from difficult times before. Participants in the financial market are now waiting to see how global policies will change later this year.
