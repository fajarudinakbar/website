Japan’s New Government and the Economy

Japan has a new Prime Minister, Sanae Takaichi. She is the first woman to lead the country. Many people are watching her carefully because she has promised to make the economy stronger. She says she will use a “responsible proactive fiscal policy.” This means she wants to spend money in smart ways to help important parts of the economy, but at the same time she wants to control Japan’s very large public debt. This is not easy, because Japan has many problems inside the country and also faces challenges from the world economy.

In recent years, Japan’s economy has not grown very much. After the Covid pandemic, growth was only about 0.8 percent each year between 2022 and 2024. In 2025, the economy grew a little faster, about 1.1 percent. This was better than before, but still not very strong. The growth came from higher incomes, government spending, and a weaker currency that made Japanese exports cheaper. However, experts believe this recovery will not last long. They expect growth to slow down again in the next two years.

One big problem is consumption. In Japan, consumption is very important because it makes up about 60 percent of the economy. If people do not spend money, the economy cannot grow. Recently, consumption has stopped growing. Inflation is high, so prices are rising faster than wages. This means families cannot buy as much as before. Real wages, which are wages adjusted for prices, have been falling. The Bank of Japan also raised interest rates to 0.5 percent. This makes borrowing money more expensive for families and businesses. It also makes it harder for the government to spend more, because debt costs are higher.

Another problem is exports. Japan sells many products to other countries, but now exports are facing new difficulties. In July 2025, Japan and the United States made a new trade agreement. This agreement put a 15 percent tariff on almost all Japanese goods entering the US. Before, the average tariff was only 1.5 percent. The US is Japan’s second largest export market after China, so this is a big problem. At the same time, global trade is slowing down because of political tensions and economic uncertainty. Since exports are about 20 percent of Japan’s GDP, this situation is very serious.

To fight these problems, Prime Minister Takaichi announced a large stimulus package worth 21.3 trillion yen. This is her first big economic plan. She hopes it will help the economy grow faster. But many experts think it will not be enough. They believe growth will slow to only 0.6 percent per year in 2026 and 2027. This is lower than the growth in 2025.

In conclusion, Japan’s new government faces many economic headwinds. Consumption is weak, exports are under pressure, and debt is very high. The Prime Minister is trying to use new policies and a big stimulus package, but the future looks difficult. Japan will need strong leadership and smart decisions to overcome these challenges.
