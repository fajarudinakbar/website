Traveller’s Tummy: Why Holidays Can Upset Your Stomach

By Health Correspondent

Many holidaymakers look forward to sunshine, new food, and relaxation. But for some, travel brings an unwelcome problem: changes in bowel habits. Doctors say “traveller’s tummy” is common, with constipation, diarrhoea, or bloating often appearing during trips abroad.

Routine Disruption

Experts explain that our digestive system prefers routine. At home, we eat and drink at regular times. On holiday, everything changes—meals, sleep, and activity levels. This sudden shift can confuse the bowels.

Constipation, defined as fewer than three bowel movements a week with hard stools, can be triggered by dehydration in hot climates, increased alcohol intake, reduced fibre, less exercise, or stress from travel. Diarrhoea, on the other hand, may result from contaminated food or water, heavy fatty meals, excess alcohol, too much fruit juice, strong coffee or tea, and long hours in the sun. Sometimes both problems occur together: diarrhoea causes dehydration, which then leads to constipation.

Prevention Tips

Health specialists recommend simple steps to avoid stomach trouble:

  • Drink plenty of water.
  • Eat fruits and vegetables similar to those at home.
  • Limit fruit juice to one glass per day.
  • Walk after meals to aid digestion.
  • Keep regular meal times and avoid skipping food.
  • Avoid very large, fatty meals.
  • Reduce alcohol intake.

What To Do If Symptoms Appear

If constipation develops, increase fluids, eat fibre-rich foods such as dried fruit or seeds, and consider mild laxatives. For diarrhoea, over-the-counter medicine like loperamide may help, but hydration is most important. Oral rehydration solutions and water are recommended. Medical advice is essential if diarrhoea includes fever, blood, or mucus.

Back to Normal

Most travellers find their bowel habits return to normal a few days after coming home. If problems continue, doctors advise seeking medical help.
