Writing Simple Introductions

Level B1 | Lesson Focus: Structure & Purpose

Part 1: Warm-up Understanding

An introduction is the first part of an academic essay. It tells the reader the topic and prepares them for the main ideas.

Part 2: What an Introduction Does

An introduction usually has two important parts: Background information and Purpose or organisation (what the essay will do).

Writing Tasks

Task 7. Controlled Writing Practice

Use the sentence frames:

  • Many young people today...
  • Recently, more people have studied...
  • This essay will explain...
Sample: “Many young people today want to stay healthy and active. Recently, more people have studied how exercise helps the body and mind. This essay will explain why exercise is important for young people.”

Task 8. Semi-guided Writing

Write an introduction (2-3 sentences) on: The advantages of learning English.

Sample: “English is used by people around the world for work and communication. Many students study English to improve their opportunities. This essay will explain the advantages of learning English.”

Task 9. Evaluating Introductions

Introduction A:

“English is fun. Many people like English. I want to write about English.”

Introduction B:

“English is an international language used in business, travel, and education. Many students learn English to communicate and study effectively. This essay will describe the advantages of learning English.”

Which one is better? Why?

Answer: Introduction B is better because it has background information, specific context, and a clear purpose statement. It is more academic and organised.

Task 10. Independent Writing Task

Write an introduction (3 sentences) for: The benefits of reading for teenagers.

Include: Background information, Specific context, Purpose statement.

Sample: “Reading is an important activity for learning and personal growth. Many teenagers read books to relax, learn new ideas, and improve their language skills. This essay will explain the benefits of reading for teenagers.”
