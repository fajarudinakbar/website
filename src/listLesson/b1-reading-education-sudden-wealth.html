Is Education a Scam? The Rise of Sudden Wealth in Indonesia

Jakarta, January 2026 — A growing debate is spreading across Indonesia as social media influencers question the value of higher education, while stories of sudden wealth attract public attention. The phrase “kuliah itu scam” (“college is a scam”) has gone viral, creating strong reactions among students, parents, and teachers.

On platforms like TikTok and Instagram, several influencers argue that university degrees no longer guarantee success. They mention high tuition fees and graduates who struggle to find good jobs. According to them, formal education feels outdated when compared to the fast money available through digital platforms. This message connects with many young Indonesians who feel disappointed by the gap between education costs and low salaries.

At the same time, stories of kaya mendadak (sudden wealth) often appear in the media. Some young people earn large amounts of money through viral videos, online trading, or digital businesses. Influencers with millions of followers show luxury lifestyles paid for by brand deals and online income. These examples make success without long years of study seem possible.

However, many educators and policymakers strongly disagree. They argue that education offers more than money. It helps people develop skills, build professional networks, and gain credibility. Research also shows that graduates usually have more stable careers in the long term. Critics warn that calling education a scam may push young people toward risky and unstable choices.

Experts say this debate reflects bigger changes in society. Young people face rising education costs, difficult job markets, and the strong attraction of digital fame. While sudden wealth stories look exciting, they often hide risks such as unstable income and poor financial planning. Education may be slow and expensive, but it remains an important investment for the future.

In conclusion, the question “Is education a scam?” highlights tension between traditional learning and new economic opportunities. Sudden wealth is rare and uncertain, while education continues to offer skills and long-term security. The choices young Indonesians make today will shape the country’s future.
