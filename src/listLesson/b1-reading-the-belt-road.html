Task 2 Vocabulary Preparation

The Belt and Road Initiative (BRI)

The Belt and Road Initiative, often called BRI, is a very large project started by China in 2013. The main goal of this project is to connect countries through better trade, transport, and communication. China offers money to build important infrastructure such as roads, railways, ports, and airports. More than 150 countries have already joined this project, and Indonesia is one of them. China also says that BRI has created hundreds of thousands of jobs around the world.

Indonesia plays a big role in BRI in Southeast Asia. It has about 70 active projects. Some examples are the Jakarta–Bandung high-speed train, nickel and smelter projects in Morowali, and the Batang Toru hydropower plant in North Sumatra. These projects show how BRI can help Indonesia improve its infrastructure and economy. For a developing country like Indonesia, access to large funding is very important, because it is often difficult to get such money from Western partners like the United States.

However, there are also challenges. Many experts warn about the risk of a “debt trap.” This means that if a country borrows too much money and cannot pay it back, it may lose control over its own projects or resources. Sri Lanka is often given as an example, because it had to deal with very high debt to China. Other risks include environmental damage and the danger of becoming too dependent on one country.

Experts in Indonesia say that BRI is not automatically negative. The key is how Indonesia manages the cooperation. If the government negotiates well and protects national interests, BRI can bring big benefits. But if Indonesia accepts everything passively, it may face economic problems in the future. Good diplomacy and smart planning are very important.
