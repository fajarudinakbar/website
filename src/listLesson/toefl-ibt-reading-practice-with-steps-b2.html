Step 2: Read for Gist and Structure

The 15-Minute City

[1] Urban planners around the world are increasingly interested in the concept of “15-minute cities.” This model proposes that residents should be able to reach most daily necessities, such as schools, workplaces, healthcare, and recreational areas, within a 15-minute walk or bicycle ride from their homes. Supporters argue that this approach improves quality of life while also addressing environmental and social challenges.

[2] One major benefit of the 15-minute city is reduced dependence on cars. When essential services are located nearby, people are less likely to rely on private vehicles. This can lead to lower traffic congestion and reduced air pollution. In addition, walking and cycling encourage physical activity, which may improve public health over time.

[3] Another advantage is stronger local communities. Because people spend more time in their neighborhoods, they are more likely to interact with local businesses and neighbors. This increased interaction can support small businesses and create a greater sense of belonging. Some studies suggest that neighborhoods designed around accessibility rather than distance tend to have higher levels of social trust.

[4] However, critics argue that implementing the 15-minute city model is not always practical. In many large cities, residential areas are already separated from commercial or industrial zones. Redesigning these cities would require significant investment and long-term planning. Furthermore, not all jobs can be relocated closer to where people live, especially specialized industries.

[5] There are also concerns about inequality. Wealthier neighborhoods may benefit more quickly from improved infrastructure, while disadvantaged areas could be left behind. If local governments fail to invest evenly, the model could unintentionally increase social divisions instead of reducing them. As a result, careful planning and policy decisions are essential to ensure that the benefits of 15-minute cities are shared fairly.
