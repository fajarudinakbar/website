General Training Academic

IELTS General Training

Focuses on everyday English skills for practical social and workplace contexts.

This module is for you if your goal is:

  • Migration: To live in an English-speaking country like Australia, Canada, New Zealand, or the UK.
  • Work Experience: To get a job or undertake work-related training abroad.
  • Below-Degree Study: To enroll in secondary education or a non-degree level vocational course.

IELTS Academic

Assesses whether you are ready to begin studying or training in an environment where English is the language of instruction.

This module is required for:

  • Higher Education: To apply for undergraduate or postgraduate courses at universities worldwide.
  • Professional Registration: To join a professional body in an English-speaking country, especially in fields like medicine, nursing, law, and engineering.

Anatomy of the Test

The full test takes 2 hours and 45 minutes. Listening, Reading, and Writing are completed back-to-back. The Speaking test is a separate interview.

Listening

~30 mins

4 sections, 40 questions. Audio is played only once.

Reading

60 mins

3 sections, 40 questions. No extra transfer time.

Writing

60 mins

2 tasks. Task 2 is worth double the marks.

Speaking

11 to 14 mins

3 parts. A one-on-one interview with an examiner.

Understanding the 9-Band Score

IELTS uses a 9-band scale to identify proficiency levels, from non-user (Band 1) to expert (Band 9). There is no pass or fail. Your overall score is an average of the four sections.

Technique Spotlight

Map Labelling: Before the audio begins, study the map. Identify the starting point and any existing labels. As you listen, follow the directional language (e.g., "turn left," "go past the library") to locate the answers.

Technique Spotlight

True / False / Not Given: Locate the specific information in the text. If the text agrees, it is True. If it says the opposite, it is False. If you cannot find the information, it is Not Given. Be careful with synonyms and qualifying words.

You are assessed on Task Achievement, Coherence & Cohesion, Lexical Resource, and Grammatical Range & Accuracy.

Task 1: Describe / Letter

For Academic, objectively summarize a graph or chart. For General, write a letter for a specific purpose, adopting the correct tone.

Task 2: Essay

Present a clear and well-supported argument in response to the prompt. A strong structure is key: Introduction, Body Paragraphs, and Conclusion.

You are assessed on Fluency & Coherence, Lexical Resource, Grammatical Range & Accuracy, and Pronunciation.

Part 1: Intro

A warm-up conversation on familiar topics like your home, work, or hobbies.

Part 2: Long Turn

You speak for 1 to 2 minutes on a topic given on a card. You have one minute to prepare.

Part 3: Discussion

A more abstract discussion related to the topic in Part 2, exploring issues in more depth.
