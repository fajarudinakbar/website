IELTS Skill-Building Worksheet

Skill: IELTS Reading

Level: B1 (Intermediate) | Focus: Understanding 'True, False, Not Given' Questions

Step 1: Awareness / Warm-up

In the IELTS Reading test, 'True, False, Not Given' (TFNG) questions test your ability to find and understand specific information.

You must read a statement and compare it to the information in the reading passage.

  • TRUE: Choose this if the statement agrees with (matches) the information in the text.
  • FALSE: Choose this if the statement contradicts (says the opposite of) the information in the text.
  • NOT GIVEN: Choose this if the information is not in the text. You cannot find it.

Look at this simple example:

Text:

"My cat, Leo, is orange and sleeps for most of the day."

  • Statement 1: The cat's name is Leo.

    Answer: True. (The text says "My cat, Leo...")

  • Statement 2: The cat is black.

    Answer: False. (The text says the cat is "orange," which contradicts "black.")

  • Statement 3: The cat likes to play.

    Answer: Not Given. (The text mentions sleeping, but it says nothing about playing. We don't know.)

Step 2: Guided Practice

A key strategy is to find the keywords in the statement first, then find that part of the text (scanning for the keywords or synonyms).

Read the text and the statements, following the guidance.

Text:

"The City Library is open from 9 a.m. to 6 p.m. from Monday to Friday. On Saturdays, it closes at 4 p.m. The library is closed on Sundays."

Statement 1: The library is open for 9 hours on weekdays.

Guidance:
1. Keywords: "9 hours," "weekdays" (which means Monday to Friday).
2. Find in text: "9 a.m. to 6 p.m. from Monday to Friday."
3. Compare: 9 a.m. to 6 p.m. is exactly 9 hours. This matches.
4. Answer: True.

Statement 2: The library is open all weekend.

Guidance:
1. Keywords: "all weekend" (which means Saturday and Sunday).
2. Find in text: "On Saturdays, it closes at 4 p.m." AND "closed on Sundays."
3. Compare: The library is not open all weekend because it is closed on one of those days. This contradicts the statement.
4. Answer: False.

Statement 3: The library has a children's section.

Guidance:
1. Keywords: "children's section."
2. Find in text: Scan the text for "children," "kids," or "section."
3. Compare: The text only talks about opening hours. It says nothing about what is inside the library. We cannot find this information.
4. Answer: Not Given.

Step 3: Controlled Practice

Read the following passage and decide if the statements (1–10) are True, False, or Not Given.

The Benefits of Urban Parks

Urban parks are essential green spaces in modern cities. They offer residents a place to relax, exercise, and meet friends. Many city parks, such as Central Park in New York, are famous tourist attractions. However, their main purpose is to improve the quality of life for local people. Studies show that spending just 20 minutes in a park can reduce stress levels. Parks also help to clean the air and provide homes for birds and insects. Maintaining these parks is expensive, but most citizens agree that the benefits are worth the cost. Many new city parks also include special areas, like dog parks or skateboarding zones, to serve different community needs.

Statements

1. Urban parks are considered very important in today's cities.

2. The only thing people do in parks is relax.

3. Central Park is located in New York.

4. The main goal of urban parks is to attract tourists.

5. Spending 20 minutes in a park can make people feel less stressed.

6. Parks can make the air quality worse.

7. The text states that parks provide homes for large animals like deer.

8. It is cheap to look after city parks.

9. Most people think that parks are a good use of money despite the cost.

10. All new parks must include a skateboarding zone.

Answer Key (Step 3)

  • 1. True. (The text says they are "essential," which means very important).
  • 2. False. (The text says they "relax, exercise, and meet friends" - more than just relax).
  • 3. True. (The text says "Central Park in New York").
  • 4. False. (The text says their "main purpose is to improve the quality of life for local people," not attract tourists).
  • 5. True. (The text says it "can reduce stress levels," which is the same as "make people feel less stressed").
  • 6. False. (The text says parks "help to clean the air," which is the opposite).
  • 7. False. (The text mentions "birds and insects," which contradicts "large animals").
  • 8. False. (The text says "Maintaining these parks is expensive").
  • 9. True. (The text says "most citizens agree that the benefits are worth the cost").
  • 10. Not Given. (The text says "many" new parks include these, but not that "all" parks must have them).

Step 4: Real Test Simulation

This passage is similar to what you might find in the IELTS General Training or Academic Reading test.

The Rise of E-readers

In recent years, e-readers—digital devices designed for reading books—have grown in popularity. Devices like the Amazon Kindle and Kobo allow users to store thousands of books on one lightweight machine. This portability is a major advantage; instead of carrying several heavy paper books for a holiday, a traveller only needs one small device.

Furthermore, many e-readers have features that traditional books lack. Users can often change the font size, look up words in a built-in dictionary, and read in the dark thanks to a backlit screen. Some newer models are also waterproof, making them ideal for reading by the pool or in the bath.

However, not everyone prefers e-readers. Some readers miss the physical feeling of a paper book—the texture of the pages and the smell of the paper. Others complain of eye strain from looking at a screen for too long, although the 'e-ink' technology used by most e-readers is designed to look like paper and reduce this problem.

Statements

Do the following statements agree with the information given in the passage? Choose True, False, or Not Given.

1. E-readers can hold more books than a standard physical bookshelf.

2. All e-reader models are waterproof.

3. Traditional paper books are usually heavier to carry than a single e-reader.

4. The text states that e-readers are now more popular than paper books.

5. 'E-ink' technology was designed to make screen reading more comfortable.

6. E-readers are more expensive than traditional books.

Answer Key (Step 4)

  • 1. True. (The text says they "store thousands of books," which is more than a standard bookshelf).
  • 2. False. (The text says "Some newer models," which means not all models are waterproof).
  • 3. True. (The text says an e-reader is lightweight and an advantage over "several heavy paper books").
  • 4. Not Given. (The text says they have "grown in popularity," but it never compares them to paper books to say which is more popular).
  • 5. True. (The text says e-ink is "designed to look like paper and reduce this problem" of eye strain).
  • 6. Not Given. (The text mentions features and popularity, but it never discusses the cost or price of e-readers compared to books).

Step 5: Reflection / Self-Assessment

Think about your practice in this worksheet:

  • Which question type was the most difficult for you: True, False, or Not Given? Why?
  • Did you get any 'False' answers confused with 'Not Given'? This is a common problem. Remember, 'False' means the text says the opposite. 'Not Given' means the text doesn't say anything about it.
  • What is your strategy if you cannot find the information in the text? (The best strategy is to mark 'Not Given' and move on quickly).
