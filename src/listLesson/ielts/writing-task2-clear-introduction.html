IELTS Writing Task 2 Clear Introduction

IELTS Skill-Building

Target Skill: Writing a Clear Introduction

Level: B1-B2 Focus: Step-by-Step Process

Instructions: Master the art of the IELTS Writing Task 2 Introduction. Work through the steps below to learn how to paraphrase prompts and write powerful thesis statements.

In IELTS Writing Task 2, your introduction is the first impression you give the examiner. It must be clear, simple, and direct. A good, clear introduction is usually just two sentences and has two essential jobs:

1

Introduce the Topic

You must show you understand the question by paraphrasing it (saying it in your own words using synonyms or changing the sentence structure).

2

Give Your Answer

You must give a clear thesis statement that tells the examiner your main opinion or outlines what the essay will discuss.

Example Breakdown

Task Prompt "Some people believe that zoos are cruel and should be closed down. To what extent do you agree or disagree?"
Simple, Clear Introduction (Good!):

Sentence 1 (Paraphrase)
"It is often argued that zoos are unkind to animals and ought to be banned."

Sentence 2 (Thesis)
"In my opinion, I completely disagree with this view because modern zoos have an important role in conservation."

Step 2A: How to Paraphrase

Paraphrasing means using different words (synonyms) or changing the sentence structure.

Prompt: "Governments should spend more money on public transport."

  • Key Words: Governments / spend more money / public transport
  • Synonyms: The state / invest in / public transportation systems

Paraphrased Sentence: "The state should invest more in public transportation systems."

Step 2B: How to Write a Thesis

Your thesis is your direct answer to the prompt. You must tailor it to the specific prompt type.

Prompt Type 1

To what extent do you agree or disagree?

Your Thesis: "In my opinion, I strongly agree with this idea."

Prompt Type 2

Discuss both views and give your opinion.

Your Thesis: "This essay will examine both sides of this argument before giving my perspective."

Useful Phrase Bank

Memorize these frameworks to build your introductions quickly and accurately.

Purpose Useful Phrases to Use
Sentence 1: Introducing the Topic
(Paraphrasing)
  • It is often argued that...
  • Many people believe that...
  • In today's world, the issue of...
  • It is a common belief that...
  • There is a growing debate about...
Sentence 2: Giving Your Thesis
(Answering the Question)
For Agree/Disagree:
  • In my opinion, I strongly [agree/disagree] with this view.
  • I believe that...
For Discuss Both Views:
  • This essay will discuss both perspectives before giving my opinion.
  • While there are arguments for [Side A], this essay will support [Side B].
For Advantages/Disadvantages:
  • This essay will explore the main [benefits/drawbacks] of this trend.
For Problems/Solutions:
  • This essay will first discuss the main causes of this problem and then propose some solutions.

Complete the following exercises using the step-by-step process and the phrase bank.

Part A: Paraphrasing Practice

Rewrite the following sentences using synonyms or different structures.

1. Many people think that children watch too much television.

Sample: "It is often argued that young people spend an excessive amount of time watching TV."

2. Studying abroad has become very popular for university students.

Sample: "Going to another country for higher education is a growing trend."

3. Fast food is unhealthy and a major cause of health problems.

Sample: "Consuming convenience food is not good for health and is a key reason for illness."

4. Living in a big city has many advantages.

Sample: "There are many benefits to residing in a large metropolitan area."

5. Social media has changed the way people communicate.

Sample: "The way individuals interact has been transformed by social networking sites."

Part B: Thesis Statement Practice

Read the prompt type and write a clear, one-sentence thesis using the Useful Phrase Bank.

6. Type To what extent do you agree or disagree? (You agree.)

Sample: "In my opinion, I strongly agree with this statement."

7. Type Discuss the advantages and disadvantages of this.

Sample: "This essay will explore the main benefits and drawbacks of this development."

8. Type Discuss both views and give your own opinion.

Sample: "This essay will discuss both sides of this argument before giving my perspective."

9. Type Is this a positive or negative development? (You think it's positive.)

Sample: "In my view, this is a largely positive development."

10. Type What are the causes of this problem, and what are the solutions?

Sample: "This essay will first discuss the main reasons for this issue and then propose some possible solutions."

Read the authentic IELTS Writing Task 2 prompt below. Write a complete, two-sentence introduction using the step-by-step process and phrases.

Task Prompt

"Many young people today leave their homes in the countryside to go and work in cities."

"Do the advantages of this trend outweigh the disadvantages?"

Write your two-sentence introduction here:

Model Introduction:

Sentence 1 (Paraphrase)
"In today's world, it is very common for young adults to move from rural areas to urban centers for employment."

Sentence 2 (Thesis)
"While this has some drawbacks, this essay will argue that the advantages of this trend are far more significant."

Look at the introduction you wrote in Step 4. Evaluate your work using the checklist below.

1. My First Sentence: Did I paraphrase the prompt by using different words and structures?

2. My Second Sentence: Is this a clear thesis statement that directly answers the prompt (about advantages/disadvantages)?

3. Useful Phrases: Did I use a phrase from the Useful Phrase Bank to help structure my ideas?

4. My Confidence: On a scale of 1 (low) to 5 (high), how confident am I that this introduction does its two jobs perfectly?
