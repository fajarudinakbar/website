IELTS Reading – Skill-Building Worksheet

Skill Focus: Decoding T/F/NG Logic

Level: B1 (Intermediate) Topic: Digital Tech in Education

Step 1: Contextual Warm-up

Scenario:

Imagine your friend says: "I bought a blue car yesterday."

Decide if the claims are TRUE, FALSE, or NOT GIVEN based only on that sentence.

1. Your friend has a new vehicle.
2. Your friend bought a red car.
3. The car was very expensive.
4. Your friend bought the car from a local dealership.

Explanations:

  • TRUE: "Bought yesterday" implies they have it now. "Car" is a "vehicle".
  • FALSE: "Blue" contradicts "Red".
  • NOT GIVEN: The price or cost is not mentioned.
  • NOT GIVEN: The place of purchase (dealership) is not mentioned.

Step 2 & 3: Strategy & Vocabulary

The Rules:

  • TRUE Matches facts (often uses synonyms).
  • FALSE Opposite or conflicting information.
  • NOT GIVEN Information is missing or we cannot be 100% sure.

Key Vocabulary:

  • Contradict: To say the opposite.
  • Absence: Information is missing.
  • Explicit: Stated clearly.
  • Inferred: Suggested but not stated directly.

Step 4 & 5: Awareness & Guided Analysis

Notice the Detail:

Text: "The new library was built using 100% recycled wood."
  • "Made of wood" → TRUE (Direct match)
  • "Built using stone" → FALSE (Contradicts 'wood')
  • "Wood was cheap" → NOT GIVEN (Recycled $\neq$ Cheap)

Guided Analysis Example:

Text: "While tablets are popular in schools, researchers found that students who use paper books remember 20% more information."

Q: Students learn better when they use paper.
Analysis: "Remember 20% more" supports "learn better". → TRUE
Q: Tablets are more expensive than paper books.
Analysis: Text says "popular", no mention of price. → NOT GIVEN

Step 6: Controlled Practice (Micro)

Select T (True), F (False), or NG (Not Given).

Text: The university was founded in 1920.

Statement: The university is over 100 years old.

Text: All students must wear a blue uniform.

Statement: Students can choose the color of their clothes.

Text: The library is closed on public holidays.

Statement: The library is open on Christmas Day.

Text: Most people prefer to study in the morning.

Statement: Everyone likes studying before noon.

Text: Research into AI began many decades ago.

Statement: AI is a brand-new technology.

Explanations:

  • TRUE: 1920 is more than 100 years ago.
  • FALSE: "Must wear" contradicts "can choose".
  • FALSE: Christmas is a public holiday, so the library is closed, not open.
  • FALSE: "Most people" does not mean "Everyone".
  • FALSE: "Decades ago" contradicts "brand-new".

Step 7: Controlled Practice (Macro)

The Impact of E-books

Many secondary schools have replaced traditional textbooks with e-readers. Proponents argue that this reduces the weight of school bags and saves money on printing. However, a small group of parents is concerned that increased screen time may lead to eye strain. To date, there is no scientific evidence in the school's report proving that e-readers cause permanent vision damage.

1. E-readers are lighter than traditional textbooks.
2. Schools save money by not printing books.
3. All parents are happy about the move to e-readers.
4. E-readers have been proven to cause permanent eye damage.
5. The school report was written by a famous scientist.

Explanations:

  • TRUE: "Reduces weight" confirms they are lighter.
  • TRUE: Text explicitly says it "saves money on printing".
  • FALSE: "A small group is concerned" contradicts "All parents are happy".
  • FALSE: Text says "no scientific evidence", statement says "proven".
  • NOT GIVEN: The author of the report is not identified as a famous scientist.

Step 8 & 9: Real Test Simulation

Tip: Watch out for "Control Words" like main reason, impossible, always.

Virtual Reality in Medicine

Virtual Reality (VR) is no longer just for gaming. Today, surgeons use VR headsets to practice complex operations before they enter the surgery room. This technology allows them to make mistakes in a safe, digital environment. While the equipment is currently very costly, many hospitals believe it will save lives in the long run. Some experts suggest that VR training is more effective than watching videos of surgery, though more research is needed to confirm this.

1. Surgeons use VR to prepare for real-life operations.
2. VR equipment is affordable for most small hospitals.
3. It is safer to practice surgery using VR than on real patients.
4. Watching videos is the most effective way to learn surgery.
5. The first VR surgery was performed in the United States.

Explanations:

  1. TRUE: Text says they "practice... before they enter the surgery room".
  2. FALSE: Text says equipment is "currently very costly" (Opposite of affordable).
  3. TRUE: Text says it is a "safe" environment to make mistakes.
  4. FALSE: Text suggests VR is "more effective" than videos (Contradiction).
  5. NOT GIVEN: The location of the first surgery is not mentioned.

Step 10: Reflection

Self-Assessment:

Action Plan:
