IELTS Speaking Skill-Building Worksheet

Level: B2 (Upper-Intermediate)

Topic: Technology and Society

Part Overview

In Speaking Part 3, you discuss broader issues connected to the Part 2 topic. Your answers should be longer than Part 1 and show clear reasoning, examples, and the ability to discuss abstract ideas.

Step 1 – Understand the Question Types

Read the questions and identify the type (Opinion, Comparison, Cause/Effect, Prediction, Evaluation).

1. How has technology changed the way people communicate?
2. Do you think modern devices make life easier?
3. What problems can technology create for young people?
4. How do you think technology will develop in the next 20 years?

Step 2 – Build Useful Language

Opinion:

  • “I tend to think that…”
  • “In my view…”

Comparison:

  • “Compared to the past…”
  • “On the other hand…”

Cause/Effect:

  • “This leads to…”
  • “As a result…”

Evaluation:

  • “One clear advantage is…”
  • “However, a major drawback is…”

Task: Choose two expressions and use them to answer:

"Do you think people depend too much on technology?"

Sample: "In my view, people definitely depend too much on gadgets. As a result, many people panic if they lose their internet connection for even a few minutes."

Step 3 – Use the P.E.E.L. Method

Structure longer answers with: Point – Explain – Example – Link

Task: "How has technology changed the way people work?"

Point (Direct Answer):

Explain (Why/How?):

Example (Specific):

Link (Conclusion):

P: Technology has fundamentally changed where we work.

E: Cloud computing allows employees to access files from anywhere.

E: For example, digital nomads can travel the world while maintaining a full-time job.

L: So, the traditional office is no longer essential.

Step 4 – Extend with B2 Connectors

Try using: Besides that, Additionally, However, On the other hand, This is mainly because...

Task: "Should schools limit the use of technology in the classroom?"

Sample: "I believe schools should limit technology to some extent. This is mainly because students can easily get distracted by games or social media. However, banning it completely isn't the answer since digital literacy is an important skill."

Step 5 – Developing Balanced Arguments

B2-level responses often show both sides before giving an opinion.

Task: "Is technology improving or harming social relationships?"

Sample: "On one hand, technology improves relationships by keeping us connected with distant friends. However, it can harm face-to-face interactions because people often look at their phones during dinner. Overall, I think it helps more than it harms if used carefully."

Step 6 – Speculating About the Future

Useful phrases: It’s possible that…, In the future, we might see…, There’s a good chance that…

Task: Predict: "How might artificial intelligence change the job market?"

Sample: "It's likely that AI will replace many repetitive jobs, such as data entry or driving. In the future, we might see a shift where humans focus more on creative and emotional tasks that machines cannot do."

Step 7 – Simulation Practice (Full Part 3)

Answer the following questions as if in the real test. Aim for 4–5 sentences per answer.

1. How has technology changed the way people learn new skills?

00:45
Technology has made learning much more accessible. People can now study through online courses and video tutorials, which means they can learn at their own pace. Additionally, many platforms offer interactive tools that make learning more engaging. As a result, more people are able to upskill without attending traditional classes.

2. What are the advantages and disadvantages of relying on smart devices?

00:45
One advantage is convenience, because smart devices help us complete daily tasks quickly. However, a major disadvantage is overreliance, which can reduce our ability to solve problems independently. Overall, they are useful tools, but people need to use them responsibly.

3. Do you think governments should regulate technology companies more strictly? Why or why not?

00:45
I tend to think stricter regulation is necessary. These companies hold a vast amount of personal data, which can be misused if not protected. Furthermore, without regulation, monopolies can form, limiting innovation. So, government oversight is crucial for user safety.

4. How do you think communication will be different in the future?

00:45
It’s possible that we will see more immersive communication technologies, like holograms or virtual reality meetings. This would make remote interactions feel almost like being in the same room. However, I hope we don't lose the value of simple face-to-face conversation.
