Part 1: Understanding Structure

Paragraph 1: Introduction
  • YES: Rewrites the task question, tells the reader what the chart shows
  • NO: No numbers, no trends, no opinions
Paragraph 2: Overview
  • YES: Gives the "big picture", summarises main trends
  • NO: No data, no small details
Paragraph 3: Body 1
  • YES: Describes the biggest changes, uses clear figures and comparisons
Paragraph 4: Body 2
  • YES: Describes smaller or stable trends, completes the data description

Band 7+ Final Practice

20:00

The bar chart compares the percentages of people who used four different types of transport, namely cars, buses, trains, and bicycles, in a city in 2000 and 2020.

Overall, there was a clear shift towards private transport over the period shown. Car usage increased substantially, while the proportion of people travelling by bus declined. In contrast, changes in train and bicycle use were relatively minor, with bicycle use remaining stable throughout the period.

The most significant changes can be observed in car and bus usage. In 2000, 45 percent of people travelled by car, and this figure rose sharply to 60 percent by 2020, making it the most popular form of transport in the later year. By contrast, bus use experienced a noticeable decline over the same period, falling from 30 percent to 20 percent, suggesting a reduced reliance on public transport.

By comparison, trends in train and bicycle use were far less dramatic. Train travel showed a modest increase, rising slightly from 15 percent to 18 percent over the 20-year period. Meanwhile, bicycle use remained unchanged at 10 percent in both years, indicating that its popularity neither increased nor decreased.
