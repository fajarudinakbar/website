IELTS Speaking Skill-Building Worksheet: Speaking Part 3

Level: B2 (Intermediate)

Focus: Justifying Opinions with Reasons and Examples

Welcome!

In IELTS Speaking Part 3, the examiner will ask you more abstract questions related to the topic from Part 2. To get a good score, you can't just give a simple opinion. You must extend your answer by explaining why you have that opinion (a reason) and giving support for that reason (an example).

This worksheet will help you practice the "Opinion ➔ Reason ➔ Example" structure to give full, well-developed answers.

Step 1 – Awareness / Warm-up

In Speaking Part 3, a strong answer always explains 'why'. Look at these two answers to the same question.

Question: "Do you think it's important for children to learn a second language?"

Answer A: "Yes, I think it's very important. All children should learn one. It's a good skill for the future."

Answer B: "Yes, I definitely believe it's crucial. [Reason] This is mainly because it opens up their minds to different cultures and ways of thinking. [Example] For instance, when my cousin learned Spanish, she didn't just learn words; she learned about Spanish festivals and traditions, which made her much more open-minded."

Task: Which answer is stronger?

Answer B is much stronger because it doesn't just state an opinion. It explains why it's important (opens minds) and gives a specific, personal example (my cousin) to prove the point. This is what you should aim for.

Step 2 – Guided Practice

Let's practice building this structure. For each question, an opinion is given. Your task is to add a logical reason and a specific example.

Question: "Do you think people rely too much on technology today?"

Opinion: "Yes, I would say we absolutely do."

Your Reason: (Think: Why do we rely on it too much? e.g., ...because it makes us lazy or we forget basic skills...)

Your Example: (Think: When have you seen this? e.g., ...take navigation, for example. Many people can't read a physical map anymore...)

Question: "What are the benefits of shopping at local markets instead of big supermarkets?"

Opinion: "In my view, the main benefit is the quality of the products."

Your Reason: (Think: Why is the quality better? e.g., ...this is due to the food being fresher and often locally grown...)

Your Example: (Think: What is a good example? e.g., ...for instance, the vegetables I buy at my local farmer's market last much longer than the ones from the supermarket...)

Step 3 – Controlled Practice

Now, practice answering these 10 questions on your own. Try to use the "Opinion ➔ Reason ➔ Example" structure for each one. Use different phrases to introduce your reasons (because..., the main reason is..., this is due to...) and examples (for example..., for instance..., a good illustration of this is...).

  1. Do you believe children should be taught how to manage money at school?
  2. What are the main disadvantages of working from home?
  3. In your opinion, is it more important to earn a high salary or to have good job satisfaction?
  4. How has social media changed the way people build friendships?
  5. Do you think governments should spend more money on public art?
  6. Why do you think some people prefer living in big cities, while others prefer the countryside?
  7. What can individuals do to help solve environmental problems?
  8. Is it better for a person to travel abroad or to explore their own country first?

  10. What are the advantages of having a hobby?

Step 5 – Reflection / Self-Assessment

Think about your performance in Steps 3 and 4. Answer these questions for yourself.

  • How many of the questions did you answer with a clear reason and example?
  • Did you find it difficult to think of specific examples?
  • Did you use the same linking phrases (like "for example") every time? What other phrases could you use? (e.g., 'To give an example...', 'A case in point is...', 'Specifically...')

🔑 Answer Key (Sample Answers)

Note: These are just examples. Your own answers, reasons, and examples are perfect as long as they are clear and logical.

Step 3 – Controlled Practice (Sample Answers)

1. Do you believe children should be taught how to manage money at school?

[Opinion] Yes, I strongly believe it should be a mandatory subject. [Reason] The main reason is that it’s a critical life skill that many parents don't teach effectively. [Example] For instance, learning how to budget, save, and understand the danger of debt at a young age could prevent many young adults from making serious financial mistakes later in life.

2. In your opinion, is it more important to earn a high salary or to have good job satisfaction?

[Opinion] In my opinion, job satisfaction is ultimately more important than a high salary. [Reason] This is because we spend such a huge portion of our lives at work, and feeling unhappy or unfulfilled every day can lead to serious stress and mental health issues. [Example] Take my uncle, for example. He left a high-paying job as a lawyer to become a teacher. He earns far less, but he says he’s never been happier because he feels he’s making a real difference.

3. What can individuals do to help solve environmental problems?

[Opinion] I think the most effective thing individuals can do is change their consumption habits. [Reason] This is due to the fact that our daily choices, when multiplied by millions of people, have a massive impact on resources. [Example] A simple example is reducing single-use plastic. By choosing to use a reusable water bottle or coffee cup, a single person can prevent hundreds of plastic items from ending up in a landfill or the ocean each year.

Step 4 – Real Test Simulation (Sample Answers)

1. How has technology changed the way students learn in your country?

[Opinion] Oh, it's changed dramatically. The biggest change I’ve seen is the access to information. [Reason] Because of the internet and tablets, students are no longer limited to just their textbooks. [Example] For instance, if a student is learning about ancient Rome, they can now take a virtual 3D tour of the Colosseum online or watch a documentary, which makes learning far more engaging than just reading a chapter in a book.

2. Some people say that students are more distracted in class because of technology like laptops and phones. To what extent do you agree?

[Opinion] I would agree to a large extent. It's a significant problem. [Reason] This is because these devices offer "instant gratification" that a traditional lesson can't always compete with. [Example] A good illustration is that even when students are supposed to be taking notes on their laptops, it's very easy for them to just switch tabs to check social media or watch videos without the teacher even noticing.

3. What are the advantages of online learning compared to traditional classroom learning?

[Opinion] The primary advantage is definitely flexibility. [Reason] People who study online can fit their education around other commitments, like a full-time job or family. [Example] For example, a working mother can't just leave her job to attend a university course from 9 to 5, but she could complete an online degree by studying in the evenings or on weekends.

4. Looking to the future, do you think teachers will be replaced by computers?

[Opinion] That's an interesting question. I would say no, I don't think they will ever be fully replaced. [Reason] The main reason is that education isn't just about transferring information; it's also about mentorship, inspiration, and emotional support. [Example] A computer can't, for instance, notice that a student is feeling discouraged and give them the personal encouragement they need to succeed. That human element is essential.
