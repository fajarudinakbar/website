Part A: How Bees Make Honey

Honey bees collect a sweet liquid called nectar from flowers. They store this nectar in a special "honey stomach." When they return to the hive, they pass the nectar to other worker bees. These bees chew the nectar for about 30 minutes. This chewing process changes the nectar into simple sugars. Next, the bees put the substance into the cells of the honeycomb. However, it is still too wet. To dry it out, the bees fan their wings to create a breeze. When the honey is ready, they seal the cell with wax to keep it clean.

Part B: Making Bricks

The process of making bricks starts with digging up clay from the ground. The clay is then crushed into a fine powder to break up any hard pieces. After that, water is added to the powder to make a thick plastic paste. This paste is pushed through a machine that shapes it into a long strip. A wire cutter then slices the strip into individual bricks. These wet bricks are not hard yet, so they are placed in a drying shed for two days. Finally, they are fired in a kiln (a hot oven) to make them strong and durable.

Recycling Glass Bottles

Glass is one of the easiest materials to recycle because it can be used again and again without losing quality. The recycling process begins when people put their used bottles and jars into recycling bins. Trucks collect these bins and transport the glass to a treatment plant.

At the plant, the glass goes onto a conveyor belt. Here, workers remove any large items that are not glass, such as plastic bags or cardboard boxes. The glass is then sorted by color: usually green, brown, and clear. This is important because different colors cannot be mixed together to make new glass.

After sorting, the glass is crushed into small pieces. These small pieces are called "cullet." The cullet must be cleaned to remove labels, glue, or metal caps. Magnets are often used to pull out any metal pieces.

Next, the clean cullet is mixed with raw materials like sand and limestone. This mixture is put into a furnace and melted at a temperature of 1500°C. The melted liquid flows into molds where it is shaped into new bottles. Finally, the new bottles are cooled down slowly to prevent them from cracking.
