Part 0: Why Surveying, Skimming, and Scanning Matter

Strong IELTS readers do NOT read every word carefully from the start. They use three complementary strategies:

1. Surveying

Purpose: Understand the structure and topic of the text before reading.

2. Skimming

Purpose: Understand the main ideas of each paragraph quickly.

3. Scanning

Purpose: Locate specific information needed to answer questions accurately.

Short Answer Questions depend mainly on scanning, but scanning only works well if you first survey and skim the text.

Part 2: Skimming for Main Ideas

Read Quickly!

Paragraph 1
As urban populations continue to grow, many cities around the world are facing increasing pressure on their transport systems. Traffic congestion, air pollution, and long commuting times have become common problems in large metropolitan areas. In response, city planners are exploring alternative forms of transport that are both environmentally friendly and cost-effective, with cycling emerging as a key solution.

Paragraph 2
Over the past decade, governments have introduced a range of measures to encourage cycling. These include the construction of dedicated bicycle lanes, investment in public bike-sharing programmes, and campaigns aimed at raising awareness of the health and environmental benefits of cycling. Such initiatives have been particularly successful among younger adults, especially those living in city centres.

Paragraph 3
Despite these developments, the growth of urban cycling has not been without challenges. Safety remains a major concern, as cyclists are often required to share roads with motor vehicles. In addition, inadequate infrastructure in some areas has limited the effectiveness of cycling policies, discouraging potential users from adopting bicycles as their main form of transport.

Paragraph 4
Looking ahead, experts argue that cycling will only become a viable long-term solution if cities continue to invest in safer infrastructure and integrate cycling into broader transport planning. Without coordinated efforts and long-term commitment, the potential benefits of cycling for urban environments may not be fully realised.

Part 5: Why Wrong Answers Fail (Example: Q2)

Incorrect Answer: "people living in city centres"

  • It uses 5 words (Limit is 2).
  • It is more specific than the main group mentioned ("younger adults").

Part 6: Strategy Reflection

Sample Reflections:

  • Surveying: Helped me understand the topic (cycling) and structure (4 paragraphs) so I wasn't confused.
  • Skimming: I knew exactly which paragraph to look in (e.g., Para 2 for "groups of people") without reading everything.
  • Scanning: Allowed me to find the exact words for the answer quickly without re-reading the whole text.
