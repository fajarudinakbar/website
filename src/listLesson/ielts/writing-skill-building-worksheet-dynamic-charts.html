Step 1 Contextual Warm-Up

Why do news reports and academic papers use line graphs? What specific information do they highlight better than a paragraph of text?

Sample Ideas:
  • To visualize changes over a specific period clearly.
  • To compare the performance or growth of different categories simultaneously.
  • To identify peaks, lows, and general trends instantly without reading data tables.

Step 3.5 Grammar Check

If a graph shows data from 2010 to 2020, which verb tense is primary? What if the graph goes up to 2050?

Strategy:
  • Past Simple: For finished time periods (e.g., 2010-2020). Example: "Sales increased."
  • Future Forms: For predictions (e.g., 2030-2050). Example: "Sales are projected to rise."
  • Present Perfect: If the data continues up to "now" (e.g., "since 2010").

Sample Task: Internet Usage (2000-2015)

Model Answer

(Introduction) The line graph illustrates the proportion of the population in the USA, Canada, and Mexico who accessed the Internet between 2000 and 2015.

(Overview) Overall, internet usage increased significantly in all three nations over the 15-year period. While Canada and the USA maintained the highest rates of connectivity throughout the timeframe, Mexico experienced the most dramatic growth relative to its starting point.

(Body Paragraph 1: High usage countries) In 2000, the USA had the highest internet penetration at approximately 70%, closely followed by Canada at 60%. Over the subsequent decade, both figures rose steadily, with Canada overtaking the USA in 2005. By 2015, internet usage in Canada had peaked at nearly 95%, whereas the USA reached a plateau of around 88% in the final years.

(Body Paragraph 2: Comparisons/Exceptions) In contrast, Mexico began the period with a much lower rate of only 20%. However, connectivity surged between 2005 and 2010, doubling to 40%. This upward trend continued, and by the end of the period, just over half of the Mexican population (55%) was online, significantly narrowing the gap with its North American neighbors.

Step 5 Guided Analysis

Look at the "Overview" paragraph in the Model Answer above. Does it mention specific numbers (e.g., 70%, 2005)? Why or why not?

Analysis:
  • No, it does NOT mention specific numbers.
  • Reason: The Overview paragraph summarizes the main trends (Overall increase, who was highest, who grew fastest). Specific data belongs in the Body Paragraphs.

Step 8a Paraphrasing the Introduction

Original Prompt: "The graph below shows the amount of electricity consumed by four different sectors in the UK from 1980 to 2020."

Sample Paraphrase: "The line chart illustrates electricity usage across four distinct industries in the United Kingdom over a 40-year period commencing in 1980."

Step 8b Grouping Information

You have 4 sectors: Residential (up), Industrial (down), Transport (up), Service (up). How would you divide your two Body Paragraphs?

Sample Grouping Strategy:
BP1: Describe the sectors that increased (Residential, Transport, Service).
BP2: Describe the sector that decreased (Industrial) to provide contrast.

Step 9 The Simulation Task

WRITING TASK 1

You should spend about 20 minutes on this task.

The graph below shows the number of visitors to a Caribbean island from 2010 to 2017. Summarise the information by selecting and reporting the main features, and make comparisons where relevant.

Outline Structure:
  • Intro: Paraphrase "number of visitors" and dates.
  • Overview: Total visitors grew; Cruise ship visitors became the most popular type by the end.
  • Details 1 (Island Stay): Started higher (0.7m), plateaued until 2013, finished lower than ships.
  • Details 2 (Cruise Ships): Started lower (0.5m), fluctuated, then surged dramatically after 2015 to overtake island stayers.
