IELTS Writing – Skill-Building Worksheet

Skill Focus: Task 2 – Essay Structure & Organization

Level: B2 (Upper-Intermediate) Topic: Social Media & Communication

Step 1: Contextual Warm-up

Prompt:

"Some people believe that social media has brought people closer together, while others think it has made our social interactions less meaningful. Discuss both views and give your opinion."

Step 2: The "Four-Paragraph" Blueprint

To achieve a high score in Coherence and Cohesion, map your ideas into four clear blocks.

1. Introduction

Paraphrase the prompt + Thesis Statement (Your main argument).

2. Body Paragraph 1 (First View)

Topic Sentence + Explanation + Example.

3. Body Paragraph 2 (Second View/Your View)

Topic Sentence + Explanation + Example.

4. Conclusion

Summarize main points + Restate final opinion.

Step 3: Vocabulary & Lexical Input

Interpersonal skills (n) The ability to communicate and interact well.
Superficial (adj) Lacking depth; only on the surface.
To bridge the gap (idiom) To reduce the distance/difference between groups.
Detrimental (adj) Tending to cause harm or damage.
Virtual community (n) A group of people who interact via the internet.
Alienation (n) The feeling of being isolated or detached.

Step 5: Analysis (The Thesis Statement)

The thesis statement tells the examiner exactly what you believe. It usually comes at the end of your introduction.

Weak Thesis:

"I will talk about both sides in this essay."

Critique: Too vague. Doesn't show your position.

Strong Thesis (Band 7+):

"While digital platforms offer unparalleled connectivity, I believe they often result in more superficial interactions compared to face-to-face contact."

Critique: Clear opinion + sophisticated vocabulary.

Model Band 8.0 Response

The digital revolution has fundamentally transformed the way humans interact, with traditional handwritten letters largely being superseded by instant messaging and email. While some argue that this shift represents a loss of personal touch and sentimentality, I believe that the practical advantages of digital communication far outweigh these minor drawbacks.

On the one hand, the decline of letter writing has led to a perceived lack of emotional depth in our exchanges. Handwritten letters are often viewed as keepsakes; they require time, effort, and a physical presence that a digital text cannot replicate. For instance, receiving a letter from a loved one often carries a sense of intimacy and value that an instant message lacks. Consequently, some people feel that modern communication has become more superficial, leading to a sense of social alienation in an increasingly fast-paced world.

However, the benefits of email and text messaging in terms of efficiency and global connectivity are unparalleled. In the past, communicating with someone in another country could take weeks or even months. Today, information can be shared instantly, which is vital for both personal emergencies and international business. Furthermore, digital communication is more inclusive and environmentally friendly. It eliminates the need for paper and transportation, and it allows individuals with speech or hearing impairments to communicate more easily through text-based platforms. For example, the ability to send instant updates via WhatsApp has helped bridge the gap for families separated by migration, maintaining bonds that might otherwise have withered.

In conclusion, although the tradition of letter writing holds significant nostalgic and emotional value, the speed, cost-effectiveness, and accessibility of modern digital tools are clearly superior. As long as users prioritize meaningful content over quantity, the transition to digital communication should be viewed as a positive evolution in human history.
