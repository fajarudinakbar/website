Mastering True / False / Not Given

Topic: Integrating Skimming and Scanning strategies.

Instructions: True/False/Not Given questions test your ability to locate specific information and understand factual details. You cannot read every word. You must learn to skim for the main idea, scan for keywords, and then read carefully to find the match.

Task 1 Cultural Connection: Reading Habits

Think about how you read a long WhatsApp message forwarded to a family group (often full of hoaxes or news). Do you read every single word from start to finish? Or do your eyes jump around looking for names, dates, and the main point? Describe your natural reading process.

Teacher's Note: When you look for specific names or dates in a WhatsApp message, you are Scanning. When you quickly read to see if the message is a joke, a warning, or news, you are Skimming. You already do this naturally; now we apply it to IELTS!

The 3-Step Reading Process

1. Skim (1-2 minutes)

Read the title, first paragraph, and first sentences of other paragraphs. Understand the gist (the main idea). Do not stop for difficult words.

2. Scan (Locate the Area)

Read the question. Underline keywords (names, dates, unique nouns). Let your eyes fly over the text to find those words or their synonyms.

3. Read for Detail (The TFNG Decision)

Once you find the sentence, read it carefully. Is it exactly the same (True)? Is it the opposite (False)? Or is the specific information missing (Not Given)?

Reading Passage: The Evolution of Jamu

Jamu, Indonesia's traditional herbal medicine, has a history spanning over a thousand years. Originally developed within the royal courts of Java, particularly during the Mataram Kingdom, these secret recipes were initially reserved exclusively for the nobility and royal family members.

It was only during the 20th century that the practice became widely commercialized and accessible to the general public. Street vendors, traditionally women carrying baskets of bottled jamu on their backs (known as jamu gendong), became a common sight in villages and cities alike.

Today, while modern Western medicine is prevalent and widely trusted, up to 80% of Indonesians still utilize jamu for various ailments, according to recent data from the Ministry of Health. It is frequently consumed for health maintenance rather than just curing sickness.

However, despite its immense domestic popularity, jamu has struggled to achieve the same lucrative global recognition as Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM). Experts suggest this is primarily due to a lack of standardized clinical trials and complex international patenting regulations, which make export difficult.

Task 3 Identifying Scanning Keywords

Look at this question: "Jamu was made available to ordinary citizens in the 19th century."
Which two words/phrases are the best keywords to scan for in the text?

Answer: You should scan for "ordinary citizens" (or public/people) and "19th century" (or dates/centuries). Names and numbers are always the best scanning targets!

Task 6 Why is it Not Given?

Look at this statement based on the text:
"Jamu is considerably cheaper to purchase than modern Western medicine."

Many students mark this as TRUE because in real life in Indonesia, Jamu is usually cheaper. Why is the correct IELTS answer Not Given?

Analysis:
The text mentions that "modern Western medicine is prevalent" and "80% of Indonesians still utilize jamu". However, there is absolutely no mention of price or cost comparison in the text.

Crucial Rule: In IELTS Reading, you must leave your general knowledge at the door. If the text does not say it, it is Not Given!

Task 7 Write the Trick

To truly understand how IELTS questions are made, you must become the examiner. Look at this sentence:

"In 2018, the government introduced a new tax on imported electronics to protect local manufacturers."

Write one FALSE statement and one NOT GIVEN statement based on that sentence.

Drafting Area
Example Statements:

FALSE: The government removed taxes on imported electronics in 2018. (It says they introduced a new tax, not removed it.)

NOT GIVEN: The new tax caused the price of local electronics to drop. (We know they wanted to protect local manufacturers, but we don't know the result on the price.)
