Moving Beyond 'PEEL': The Concession-Refutation Model

To achieve Band 8/9, examiners want to see that you understand the complexity of an issue. Instead of just listing points supporting your side, you should acknowledge the opposing view (Concession), and then systematically dismantle it (Refutation).

Step 1: The Concession
Acknowledge the validity of the opposing argument.
"Admittedly, proponents of massive infrastructure projects argue that..."
Step 2: The Pivot
Introduce your counter-argument smoothly.
"Nevertheless, this perspective is fundamentally flawed because..."
Step 3: The Refutation
Assert your main position with powerful logic.
"It ignores the irreversible ecological damage that supersedes short-term financial gain."
Step 4: Evidence & Synthesis
Provide concrete evidence proving your refutation.
"For instance, the rapid deforestation in Kalimantan illustrates..."

Where to Place the Concession Paragraph?

In a standard 4-paragraph essay, strategic placement of this framework is critical for coherence. There are two highly effective options:

  • Option A: Body Paragraph 1 (The "Clear the Air" approach). Use the Concession-Refutation model immediately after your introduction. Acknowledge the opposing view and dismantle it. This clears the path for Body Paragraph 2, where you build your main, strongest arguments without interruption.
  • Option B: Body Paragraph 2 (The "Bulletproof" approach). Establish your main argument strongly in Body Paragraph 1. Then, use Body Paragraph 2 for the Concession-Refutation to show the examiner you have considered alternative views but your stance remains superior before moving to the conclusion.

Band 9 Model Paragraph Analysis

Prompt: Economic progress is often used to measure a country's success. However, some people believe that other factors are more important. Discuss both views and give your opinion.

CONCESSION It is often argued by advocates of sheer economic growth that GDP is the most quantifiable metric of a nation's prosperity. To a certain extent, this perspective holds validity; without robust economic foundations, governments simply cannot fund essential public services such as universal healthcare or advanced education.

PIVOT & REFUTATION Notwithstanding these benefits, reducing a country’s success to mere financial output is a profoundly narrow paradigm. This approach critically ignores less tangible but equally vital indicators of societal well-being, such as environmental sustainability and civil liberties.

SYNTHESIS For example, a nation might boast a soaring GDP generated by rampant industrialization, while simultaneously suffering from catastrophic ecological degradation and severe wealth inequality. Such a dichotomy hardly reflects true 'success', demonstrating that economic metrics must be balanced with holistic human development indexes.

Task 3 Upgrading Grammar (Cleft Sentences)

A "Cleft Sentence" is used for emphasis at C2 level (e.g., "It is the government that must act." instead of "The government must act."). Rewrite the following B2 sentence into a Cleft Sentence:

"Education determines a nation's future, not just wealth."

Answer: "It is education, rather than mere wealth, that truly determines a nation's future." (This structure guarantees high marks for Grammatical Range).

Task 6 The Pivot Mastery

Combine the following two conflicting ideas into one highly complex sentence using a concession pivot (e.g., While X is true, it fails to account for Y).

Idea 1: Online learning offers unparalleled convenience for remote students.

C2 Model Paragraph:

Admittedly, engaging teenagers in community service yields undeniable benefits; it fosters empathy, builds social cohesion, and broadens a student's worldview beyond the academic curriculum. However, the fundamental flaw in making such programmes compulsory lies in the very nature of volunteering itself. By mandating unpaid work, schools strip away the element of intrinsic motivation, effectively transforming a noble civic duty into an resented obligation. Consequently, rather than cultivating a genuine desire to assist the marginalized, forced participation often breeds apathy and resentment among overburdened students. For instance, a student coerced into working at an animal shelter merely to fulfill a graduation requirement is unlikely to develop a lifelong commitment to animal welfare, thereby defeating the programme's primary objective. Ultimately, community spirit must be inspired, not legislated.
