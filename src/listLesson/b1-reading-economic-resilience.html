Reading Passage

The world economy today is full of problems. Conflicts between countries continue, energy prices go up and down, and many central banks keep interest rates high. Because of this, money moves quickly from one place to another. Many developing countries feel pressure on their currency, investment, and growth.

Indonesia is not completely safe from these problems. But compared to many other countries, Indonesia's economy is still strong. Growth stays around 5 percent, inflation is under control, and trade balance shows a surplus. Fiscal and monetary policies also help keep STABILITY.

For young people, this is good news, but it does not mean the challenges are finished. The question now is: can Indonesia use this strong base to create new opportunities?

Uncertainty Is the New Normal

Economist Enrico Tanuwidjaja from UOB said that uncertainty is now part of global life. "Nothing is certain except uncertainty itself," he explained. Prices of energy can rise quickly, which makes inflation higher. Then central banks raise interest rates, and growth slows down.

Even so, Indonesia is still in a better position than many countries. Growth is higher than the world average, supported by strong DOMESTIC consumption. But Enrico reminded that 5 percent growth is not enough. "We are still young. We should be able to run faster," he said.

Demographic Bonus Needs Action

Indonesia now enjoys a demographic bonus. Most people are in PRODUCTIVE age. This can be a golden chance to increase productivity, consumption, and investment. But this chance will not come automatically.

Enrico said Indonesia must build new engines of growth. Three things are important: good quality government spending, long-term investment, and stronger exports. At the same time, technology and artificial intelligence are changing industries quickly. This means Indonesia must also improve the quality of its human resources.

Gen Z Must Do More Than Consume

Data shows that many young people are still not active in investment. For example, Gen Z has small PARTICIPATION in government retail bonds. This shows that financial literacy is still low.

Enrico explained that Gen Z will be the largest group in Indonesia's economy in the future. So they cannot only be consumers. They must also build ASSETS, improve skills, and understand how the economy works. This will make them more productive and ready for future challenges.

Building Strength from Inside

Indonesia also has strong LIQUIDITY in banks. This means there is money available to support productive sectors. The challenge is to build trust so that funds go to industries that create jobs.

Enrico said manufacturing must be strengthened again. Indonesia should not move too fast into a service economy without a strong industrial base. A weaker rupiah is not always bad. If inflation is controlled, a competitive currency can help exports and tourism.

The most important thing is confidence. Trust from investors, businesses, and society is the main capital for higher growth. Stable macroeconomic conditions are only the beginning. The future depends on how Indonesia uses its demographic bonus, improves human resources, and prepares young people for change.

Global uncertainty may continue. But if Indonesia keeps stability and increases productivity, the chance to grow faster is still open. For Gen Z, the best strategy is to start building skills and capacity now, so they are ready when opportunities arrive.
