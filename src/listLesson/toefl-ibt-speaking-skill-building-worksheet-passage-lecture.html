Task Type: Integrated Speaking (Task 3)

Skill Focus: Explaining an academic concept

Step 1: Understand Speaking Task 3

In Speaking Task 3, you will:

  • Read a short academic definition.
  • Listen to a lecture that gives an example.
  • Explain the concept and show how the example illustrates it.

Note: You do not give your personal opinion.

Step 2: Read the Academic Definition

Planned Obsolescence

Planned obsolescence is a business strategy in which products are designed to have a limited lifespan. Companies intentionally make products that will become outdated or stop working after a certain period of time. This encourages consumers to replace products more frequently, increasing company profits.

Step 4: Listen for the Example

Listen to the professor explain the concept using a specific example.

Lecture Audio

Professor: "Let's look at a common example of this strategy: smartphones. You see, many smartphones are designed in a way that their batteries cannot be easily replaced by the user. After a few years, the battery performance naturally becomes much worse—it doesn't hold a charge. But, because you can't just swap in a new battery, what happens? Instead of repairing the phone, users often just go out and buy a brand new one. This is a clear example of planned obsolescence because the product is intentionally designed to be replaced, not repaired."

Step 6: Build the Speaking Structure

A strong Task 3 response follows this structure:

  1. State the concept.
  2. Explain the definition (Reading).
  3. Introduce the example (Lecture).
  4. Explain how the example illustrates the concept.

Useful Language:

  • "The reading defines [concept] as..."
  • "In the lecture, the professor gives an example of..."
  • "This is an example of [concept] because..."
