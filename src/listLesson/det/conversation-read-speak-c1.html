Read, Then Speak

Score Range: 120–135 | Focus: Fluency, Coherence & Complexity

Instructions: Master the 90-second speaking task. Read the prompt, prepare quickly, and speak extensively. The prompt remains visible while you speak.

Step 1 The Concept Breakdown

The Task: Respond verbally (90 seconds) to a written prompt describing an object, situation, or opinion.

The Goal: Address every bullet point in the prompt while maintaining fluency and complexity.

Step 2 Recognition Drill

Experience-Based

"Describe a time you worked with others. What did you collaborate on? Do you enjoy it?"

Opinion-Based

"Describe changes you would make in the world and steps you would take."

Description-Based

"Talk about a book you read recently. Title? Plot? Why did you like it?"

Step 3 Strategic Roadmap

1. Address Every Bullet Point: Use the prompt questions as your speech outline to maximize Content scores.
2. Maximize Time: Aim for the full 90 seconds. If you finish early, elaborate or conclude.
3. Leverage Prep Time: Use the 20-second prep window to mentally map your first sentence.

Step 5 Guided Practice (Scaffolded)

Task:

"Discuss the influence of television on young people. What are the potential benefits and risks?"

Thinking Prompts:
  • Educational: Science, languages?
  • Risks: Health, unrealistic standards?
  • Summary: "Double-edged sword"?

"I believe that television can have both a positive and negative influence on young people. On the positive side, TV can be an informative tool, helping youth learn about different cultures and subjects like science or languages. However, the biggest risks involve their physical and mental health. Excessive screen time can lead to sedentary lifestyles. Furthermore, programs often promote unrealistic beauty standards, which can affect mental well-being. Basically, television is a double-edged sword; while beneficial, young people need mindful viewing habits."

Why it Works: Cohesive devices (On the positive side, Furthermore), Lexical variety (sedentary, mindful), Complex structure.

Step 6: Technical Simulation

  • Eye Contact: Do not look away from the screen/camera.
  • Timer: 90 seconds continuous speaking.
  • Environment: Silent room, good lighting.
