Read, Then Speak Mastery

Score Range: 100–120 | Focus: Syntactic Variety & Lexical Range

Instructions: Master the 90-second speaking task. The test evaluates your ability to express viewpoints clearly and sustain a coherent argument. You have 2 attempts per written drill before sample answers are revealed.

Step 1: The Deep-Dive Breakdown

The "Read, Then Speak" task requires you to read a prompt and speak for 30 to 90 seconds (with exactly 20 seconds to prepare). At B2, the test is looking for a "vantage point" speaker who can express viewpoints clearly.

  • Subscore Impact: Production and Conversation.
  • Linguistic Requirement: Scans for Syntactic Variety (simple + complex sentences) and Lexical Range (topic-specific vocab). Evaluates Acoustic Features (pacing, intonation).

Common Pitfalls:

  • The "Silent Prep" Error: Writing full sentences during the 20s prep instead of keywords.
  • Prompt Neglect: Failing to address every bullet point.
  • Monotonous Delivery: Speaking in a flat tone hurts pronunciation/fluency scores.

Step 2: The Recognition & Analysis Drill

Examine these five typical B2 prompts:

  • Describe a time you had to learn a new skill. What was it, and how did you feel?
  • Talk about a place you would like to visit in the future. Why does it interest you?
  • Some people prefer to work in teams, while others prefer to work alone. Which do you prefer?
  • Describe a piece of technology that has changed your life.
  • Who is a person you admire, and what qualities do they possess?

Step 3: The Strategic Roadmap (Pro-Tips+)

"Power Opening"

Don't repeat the prompt word-for-word. Paraphrase it to boost your "Unique Word Count".

The PEEL Structure

Organize using Point, Evidence, Explanation, Link. Prevents "circling" behaviors.

Signposting

Use "Moving on to..." or "To illustrate this...". Helps the algorithm identify transitions.

Emphasize Content Words

Slightly stress nouns, verbs, and adjectives. Keep function words light for natural rhythm.

Keep Speaking (75s+)

Volume is a proxy for fluency. Short responses fail to showcase enough complex grammar for B2/C1.

Drill C: The "Grammar Upgrade"

Change this simple sentence sequence into a B2 complex sentence:

"I went to the park. It was raining. I had fun."

Prompt

"Describe a traditional food from your country. What is it made of, and when do people usually eat it?"

Mental Modeling Prompt:

Prep thoughts: I'll choose Tacos. Ingredients: tortillas, meat, salsa. Occasions: parties, every day. I need to use words like "staple," "cultural heritage," and "versatile."

Comparative Models:

Passing (B1/B2):

"I want to talk about tacos. They are from Mexico. They are made with tortillas and meat. People eat them every day. I like them because they are very delicious and spicy. They are famous all over the world."

Exceptional (B2/C1):

"A quintessential dish from my country is the taco, which is essentially a cultural staple. It consists of a corn or wheat tortilla filled with various proteins, topped with fresh cilantro and spicy salsa. Interestingly, while it is consumed daily, it truly takes center stage during national celebrations. I believe its popularity stems from its incredible versatility."

Linguistic Spotlight: The exceptional response uses Adverbs ("essentially," "interestingly"), Relative Clauses ("which is..."), and Advanced Collocations ("cultural staple," "takes center stage," "popularity stems from").

Preparation Phase

Level 1: Standard (Personal)

"Describe a hobby you enjoy. How did you get started, and why do you find it interesting?"

Key Strategy: Focus on "Initial Spark" (How I started) and "Intrinsic Motivation" (Why it's interesting). Avoid just listing equipment.

Level 2: Complex (Opinion-based)

"Do you think it is better to live in a small town or a big city? Give reasons for your choice."

Key Strategy: Use "Comparative Structures" (In contrast to, significantly more, whereas) to show higher-level syntax.

Level 3: Academic/Abstract (Societal)

"In many cultures, traditions are disappearing. Do you think this is a positive or negative development? Explain why."

Key Strategy: Use "Hypothetical Language" (If we lose our traditions, we might..., it could be argued that...).
