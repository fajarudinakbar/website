The Trend of “10,000 Rupiah in the Wife’s Hands”

In October 2025, social media in Indonesia was busy with a new trend. A video showed a pregnant wife who received only 10,000 rupiah from her husband for daily shopping. She bought food for 6,000 rupiah and saved the rest, 4,000 rupiah. Many people reacted strongly. They said this story was not realistic in today’s economy.

This trend is called “10,000 in the wife’s hands.” It looks like a romantic story about a smart wife who can manage money well. But behind the story, there are bigger problems. It shows how wives are often given too much responsibility in the family.

The Role of the Wife

In the video, the wife is shown as the person who must make sure the family can eat, no matter how little money she gets. This idea is not new. It comes from old beliefs in Indonesia, especially during the New Order period. At that time, the government promoted the idea of “state motherhood.” This means wives should take care of the home, serve the community, and even serve the nation.

Because of this, many people think the “ideal wife” is someone who can always manage the household, even in very hard times. But this is unfair. It makes wives carry the burden alone, while husbands are not asked to share the responsibility equally.

The Economic Situation

The trend also hides another problem: the difficult economy in Indonesia. Prices of food, housing, and education are rising. Many families, especially middle and lower-class families, struggle to survive.

When the video says that a wife should be creative and save money, it suggests that the problem is only about the individual. But in reality, the problem is bigger. It is about government policies, public services, and social inequality.

For example, school fees are high, health services are not equal, and housing is expensive. Many people also depend on online loans or “pay later” systems, which make life harder. These are structural problems, not just personal ones.

Neoliberal Ideas

The focus on individual responsibility is part of neoliberalism. Neoliberalism is an economic idea that says each person must take care of themselves. If someone is poor, it is seen as their own failure.

This way of thinking ignores the role of the government. It also ignores the need for strong public services. Instead, people are told to learn financial management or “frugal living.” But these solutions cannot solve the real problems of inequality and poverty.

Old Narratives, New Problems

The idea of “10,000 in the wife’s hands” is not new. It appeared before, in 2023. Now it comes back, mixed with patriarchy. Patriarchy is a system where men have more power than women. In this system, the “happy housewife” is seen as the ideal figure.

This makes many women accept unfair roles as normal. They are told that managing the home is their duty and even their happiness. But in reality, it is also a way to hide the government’s failure to provide good services.

What Can Be Done?

There are two levels of solutions. In the family: Husbands and wives should share responsibilities fairly. Cooking, shopping, and managing money should not be only the wife’s job. Families should work together to survive economic challenges. Communities can also help each other, like during the COVID-19 pandemic when people shared food.

In government policy: The government must improve public services. It should not only focus on efficiency but also on quality. Fair taxes, better social protection, and programs like Universal Basic Income (UBI) can help. UBI means the government gives money to all citizens to cover basic needs. This can support women, informal workers, and vulnerable groups.

Conclusion

The trend of “10,000 in the wife’s hands” looks sweet and romantic. But it hides serious problems. It shows unfair gender roles and ignores the real economic difficulties in Indonesia. Families need equality, and the government must take responsibility. Only then can society face the challenges of today’s economy.
