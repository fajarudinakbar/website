TOEIC Reading B1-B2 Question Types

Reading Section Overview

Topic: Navigating Parts 5, 6, and 7

Instructions: This worksheet uses the 10 Step Drill Master approach to build reading stamina across all question types. Complete the tasks sequentially. For interactive quizzes, you have two attempts before the correct answer, explanation, and citation source are revealed.

Real Test Format: Part 5

In the real test, you will read a sentence with a missing word or phrase. You must select the best answer to complete the sentence. Four options (A, B, C, D) are printed below each sentence. These questions test your knowledge of vocabulary and grammar.

Strategy: The 2 Pass Method
  • Time management is an important part of doing the reading test.
  • You should spend no more than about 30 to 45 seconds on each Part 5 and 6 question.
  • On the first pass, quickly answer the easy questions.
  • On the second pass, go back and spend a bit more time on the more challenging questions.
  • If you do not know the answer, guess and move on.

Step 1 Pattern Identification

A common TOEIC Part 5 trap involves Word Families. First, decide what part of speech is needed (noun, verb, adjective, etc.) by looking at the surrounding sentence. You can use suffixes (word endings) to help identify the part of speech. Look at this example:

The manager relies on Susan because she always completes her work __________.

Explain how to quickly identify the correct grammatical structure for the blank without needing to read the entire sentence.

Pattern Analysis:

In Word Family questions, look at the surrounding words. The blank comes after a complete clause and describes the verb "completes". Verbs are modified by adverbs. Therefore, the adverb form (e.g., "reliably") is the only grammatically correct choice. Looking closely at suffixes quickly eliminates incorrect parts of speech.

Step 2 Part 5 Speed Drill

You have 60 seconds to identify the correct grammatical structure or vocabulary word for all 5 items. Remember your 2 Pass Method!

Real Test Format: Part 6

In the real test, you will read a short text (like a letter or memo) that has four blanks. You must select the best word, phrase, or entire sentence to complete the text. Four options are printed for each blank.

Strategy: Verb Forms and Contextual Clues
  • Some questions provide four options that feature different tenses of the same verb.
  • Look at the sentence to decide what tense is required, and then choose the correct option.
  • Sometimes the sentence may not give enough information to choose the correct answer.
  • In this case look at the rest of the text to find the correct option.

Step 3 & 4 Contextual Analysis Drill

To: All Staff

From: IT Department

Date: November 1

Subject: System Update

Please note that the company servers will be undergoing scheduled maintenance this weekend. The system will go offline on Friday at 10:00 PM and will remain unavailable until Sunday morning. (1) _________ . We apologize for any (2) _________ this may cause.

Please ensure you save all your work locally before the shutdown. If you experience issues logging in on Monday, please (3) _________ the IT helpdesk immediately.

Real Test Format: Part 7 (Single)

In the real test, you will read a variety of single texts (emails, articles, notices, advertisements). Each text is followed by 2 to 4 questions. You must select the best answer based solely on the information provided in the text.

Strategy: Question Prioritization
  • You should spend no more than about 60 to 90 seconds on each Part 7 question.
  • To save time, do not start reading the passage until you know exactly what you need to find.
  • Answer questions in the most efficient order.
  • Do specific information questions first.
  • Pick out key words (nouns and verbs) in the question.
  • Scan the passage for the key words or ideas, then choose the best answer choice.

Step 5 & 6 Comprehension Check

Notice of Office Relocation

Starting December 1, Greenfield Financial will be moving its headquarters from 14th Street to a larger facility at 89 West Avenue. The new location features a larger client reception area and expanded parking. Our phone numbers and email addresses will remain the same.

The office will be closed on November 30 to facilitate the move. We look forward to serving you at our new location.

Real Test Format: Part 7 (Multiple)

In the real test, you will read sets of two or three related texts (for example, a schedule followed by an email). You must answer 5 questions per set. Many of these questions require you to cross-reference and synthesize information from more than one text.

Strategy: Managing Complex Questions
  • Vocabulary questions should be answered quickly.
  • If you do not know the word or words, guess and move on.
  • Doing specific information and vocabulary questions first will help prepare you for main idea or inference questions.
  • Negative specific information questions (like "Which of the following is NOT true?") can be the most time consuming.
  • Leave them until last, when you may have already got information to help you with the answers.

Step 7 & 8 Information Synthesis

Text 1: Memo

To: All Staff

From: Julian Bates, Facilities Management

Date: October 12

Subject: 3rd Floor Office Renovations

Please be advised that the planned renovations for the 3rd floor marketing wing will commence on Monday, October 20. The construction crew will be upgrading the flooring, painting the walls, and installing new modular workstations. This project is expected to last for exactly two weeks.

All personnel currently seated on the 3rd floor must pack up their personal belongings and empty their filing cabinets by the end of the day on Friday, October 17. Boxes will be provided by the facilities team starting Wednesday.

While the renovation is underway, affected employees will be relocated temporarily to the grand conference room on the 1st floor. We apologize for any inconvenience this mandatory update may cause and thank you for your cooperation as we improve our workspace.

Text 2: E-mail

To: Julian Bates <jbates@corpmail.com>

From: Sarah Jenkins <sjenkins@corpmail.com>

Date: October 13

Subject: Re: 3rd Floor Office Renovations

Dear Mr. Bates,

I am writing regarding the upcoming renovations in the marketing wing. I will be out of the office attending the regional sales summit from Thursday, October 16 through Tuesday, October 21. Because of this, I will not be able to pack my desk on the deadline specified in your memo.

Would it be possible for me to receive my boxes earlier so I can pack my desk before I leave on Wednesday afternoon? Furthermore, as my team relies heavily on cloud-based design software, I wanted to confirm if the temporary workspace will be equipped with high speed internet access.

Thank you for accommodating my request.

Best regards,
Sarah Jenkins
Senior Marketing Coordinator

Step 9 Workplace Transformation

In the Part 7 memo, Mr. Bates writes: "All personnel currently seated on the 3rd floor must pack up their personal belongings and empty their filing cabinets by the end of the day on Friday." Rewrite this directive to sound softer and more polite, as if it were a gentle reminder to your colleagues.

Sample Polite Transformation:
  • "We kindly request that all 3rd floor staff pack their belongings and empty their cabinets by Friday."
  • "Please remember to pack up your personal items by the end of the day on Friday."

Step 10 Strategic Reflection

Reflecting on the Reading section, how does explicitly teaching students to identify document types (e.g., distinguishing a formal memo from a casual email) improve their scanning speed in Part 7?

Reflection Insight:

Different document types have predictable structures. Memos usually state the problem immediately in the first paragraph, while emails often have a specific request near the end. By recognizing the format, students know exactly where to scan for specific information, reducing reading fatigue and saving crucial time.
