Real Test Format: Part 1

In the real TOEIC test, you will see a photograph in your test book. You will hear four short statements about the photograph. These statements are spoken only once and are NOT printed. You must select the statement (A, B, C, or D) that best describes the picture.

Strategy: Predicting and Analyzing
  • As soon as this section starts, pick out the main theme of the first photograph and start to predict the type of statements you may hear.
  • Brainstorm nouns and verbs related to the picture before you listen.
  • Listen for SVO (subject, verb, object) words, as most incorrect choices will use some correct subject, verb, and object words and some wrong ones.
  • Many of the incorrect answer choices in this section feature an inappropriate verb for the situation.

Step 1 Diagnostic

Without checking notes, briefly describe what test takers must do in Part 1 of the TOEIC Listening test. What are the common traps to avoid?

Diagnostic Outline:
  • Task: You will hear four statements about a picture. You must select the one statement that best describes what you see. The statements are not printed.
  • Common Traps: Distractors often involve the wrong noun or verb, or use some correct subject, verb, and object words paired with incorrect ones.

Real Test Format: Part 2

In the real test, you will hear a question or statement followed by three responses spoken in English. They will NOT be printed in your test book and are spoken only once. You must choose the best response (A, B, or C) based purely on your listening comprehension.

Strategy: Meaning over Sound
  • Answers in the TOEIC test do not always answer the question directly.
  • Often the question and answer will be different tenses.
  • Often direct questions will not be answered with yes, no or don't know, so look for options that use different words to express these meanings.
  • Be careful when choosing responses that use the same or similar sounding words, as distractors using the same or similar sounding words are common in Part 2.

Step 4 Pattern Identification

In Question 7 above, the distractor "I will send a fax tomorrow" uses word repetition to trick test takers. Why does this strategy work, and how can you teach students to avoid it?

Pattern Analysis:

It works because low-level listeners panic when they miss the Wh- word (Where) and cling to the only noun they recognized (fax). Distractors using the same (or similar sounding) words are common in Part 2. To avoid it, students must drill focusing on meaning rather than matching sounds.

Real Test Format: Part 3

In the real test, you will hear a conversation between two or more people spoken only once. Unlike Parts 1 and 2, the questions and the four answer choices ARE printed in your test book. You must read along and select the best response based on the audio.

Strategy: Prediction and Distractors
  • Using the time available to skim the questions and answer choices before listening will help you to identify the key parts of the conversation.
  • Part 3 sometimes uses the same words in the recording and answer choices, but with a different meaning.
  • Listen to who says what, as often the answer choice will have key words used by one of the speakers, but it may not be the speaker specified in the question.
  • Sometimes the answers are not stated directly in the conversation, so look for common inference markers such as "What can be said", "implied", or "inferred".

Step 5 Contextual Listening

Questions 32 through 34 refer to the following conversation.
"Hello. I'm calling about a coffee machine I purchased from your Web site. It stopped working even though I haven't had it for very long. I expected it to last much longer than this."
"Oh, I'm sorry to hear that. Our warranty covers products for up to a year. Do you know when you bought it?"
"I've had it for a little over a year, so the warranty has probably just expired. This is so disappointing."
"Well, I'll tell you what we can do. Although we can't replace it, since you're a valued customer I can offer you a coupon for forty percent off your next purchase."

Real Test Format: Part 4

In the real test, you will hear a short talk given by a single speaker, spoken only once. Just like Part 3, the questions and the four answer choices ARE printed in your test book. You must use the printed options to answer questions about the audio.

Strategy: Following the Sequence
  • Answers in Part 4 usually appear in the order in which they appear in the talks.
  • Some "what" questions require you to understand the main idea, while others require you to listen for specific information.
  • The correct answer choice often uses different words from what you will hear, so be aware of this and listen for meaning, not just the key words.
  • Be careful of questions involving number and quantity, and be careful of sound distractors in numbers ending in "teen" or "ty".

Step 9 Workplace Transformation

In Part 3, the woman says: "It stopped working even though I haven't had it for very long." Rewrite this sentence to sound professional, as if she were writing a formal complaint email to Customer Service.

Sample Formal Transformation:
  • "The appliance ceased functioning shortly after the date of purchase."
  • "Despite being recently acquired, the machine has unexpectedly malfunctioned."

Step 10 Strategic Reflection

Reflecting on the entire Listening section, how can integrating authentic corporate documents (like warranties and emails) into your lessons improve your students' listening comprehension scores?

Reflection Insight:

Authentic materials build schema. When students read actual warranties or memos, they anticipate the vocabulary and context required in Parts 3 and 4. This bridges the gap between passive listening and active, real world application, directly aligning with communicative teaching principles.
