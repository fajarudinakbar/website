Task 2 Vocabulary in Context

Fraud is a crime that hurts many people in Indonesia.

Every year, new cases appear with different tricks. Victims lose money, but most of the time they do not get it back. Even when the criminal is caught and punished, the victims still face big losses. Why does this happen?

Focus on Punishing Criminals

In Indonesian law, fraud is treated differently from corruption. In corruption cases, the state is the victim. The law is designed to recover money for the state. Assets can be taken and returned to the government. But in fraud cases, the victims are individuals or groups. The state acts only as law enforcer. That means the criminal can go to jail, but the victims’ money does not automatically return. Victims must use civil lawsuits or restitution, which is often very difficult.

Examples of Fraud Cases

There are many famous cases. In 2007, the travel company First Travel promised cheap trips for umrah. More than 63,000 people lost money, around Rp900 billion. The court decided to take the assets, but they were not returned quickly to the victims. Even after years, the process of selling the assets is still not finished.

Another case is Ayu Puspita, a wedding organizer. She used money from clients to buy a house. When she was arrested, the money was already gone. Victims were left with nothing.

Recently, Timothy Ronald, an influencer and founder of a crypto academy, was accused of cheating investors. One victim said he lost Rp3 billion. The police are still investigating. These examples show that fraud keeps changing its face, but the result is the same: victims rarely get full justice.

Problems with Asset Recovery

One reason victims do not get their money back is because criminals spend it quickly. They use it for old victims, for promotion, or for their lifestyle. When the police catch them, only a small part of the money is left. Assets like cars or houses lose value over time. When they are sold, the price is lower than the original. The process of selling assets is also slow and complicated. In big cases with thousands of victims, it takes years to check all claims. In the end, victims receive only a small part of their loss.

Why Fraud Never Disappears

Fraud continues because people still hope for easy profit. Economic pressure, lack of knowledge, and promises of quick money make people vulnerable. Technology also helps fraud spread faster. Social media and digital platforms allow criminals to reach many victims in a short time. Police can only act after the crime happens. That is why prevention is more important than punishment.

What Can Be Done

Experts say that the best protection is awareness. People must be careful with offers that sound too good. Financial literacy is important. The government can also improve rules, for example by separating victims’ money earlier in investigations or using escrow accounts in some services. But the main lesson is clear: the law punishes criminals, but it does not guarantee that victims will get their money back. Prevention is the safest way.
