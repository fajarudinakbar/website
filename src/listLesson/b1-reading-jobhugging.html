Embracing Stability in Uncertain Times

In recent years, many young workers in Indonesia have started talking about a new trend called "job hugging." This phrase means staying in the same job for a long time, even if the job is not perfect. People choose to hold on to their current position because they feel safer, especially when the economy is uncertain.

In the past, the popular trend was "job hopping." Job hopping means moving from one company to another to find better pay, healthier workplaces, or bigger career opportunities. Young professionals often believed that changing jobs quickly was the best way to grow. But now, things are different. With the economy slowing down and many companies reducing staff, workers feel that staying in one place is less risky.

A researcher, Norman Luther Aruan, explains that this situation is not only happening in Indonesia. Workers in many countries are also choosing to stay in their jobs. The global economy is unstable, and technology is changing very fast. Automation and digital tools are replacing some jobs, so workers must keep learning new skills to stay useful.

Norman points out that many workers appear loyal to their companies, but inside they often feel tired, stressed, or unmotivated. Employers may think these workers are happy, but in reality, they are only staying because they fear losing their income. This can create problems. When workers do not feel motivated, they cannot give their best performance, and companies may suffer in the long run.

He believes that workers need two important things to stay strong and productive:

  • Security: Workers want to feel safe about their income and job stability.
  • Opportunities to grow: Workers need chances to learn new skills, develop their careers, and feel that their work has meaning.

Without these two elements, it will be difficult to build a workforce that can support the country's economy. Workers may stay in their jobs, but they will not be truly engaged.

For example, imagine a young professional who wants to improve their skills but sees no training opportunities in their company. They may continue working there because they need money, but they will not feel excited or motivated. On the other hand, if the company offers training, mentoring, or career development programs, the worker will feel more positive and ready to contribute.

The idea of job hugging shows how economic conditions can change people's behavior. When times are good, workers may feel free to move around and search for better opportunities. But when times are hard, they prefer to stay where they are, even if they are not fully satisfied.

In conclusion, job hugging is a sign of today's uncertain world. Workers are choosing safety over growth, but this choice has limits. To build a strong economy, companies must give workers both security and opportunities. Only then will workers feel loyal, motivated, and ready to face the challenges of the future.
