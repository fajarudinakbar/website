Guiding Visitors

Topic: Writing clear location descriptions for colleagues and visitors

Instructions: When writing an orientation email to a visiting medical colleague, your main goal is clear communication. They need to understand the space quickly. In this lesson, we will deconstruct an official campus orientation guide and practice writing our own helpful, clear directions.

Task 1: Phrases for Clear Directions

To write a helpful guide, you must use precise phrases so the reader can visualize the space. Brainstorm five phrases you would use to tell a visitor where a building is located on a map.

Clear, Helpful Phrases:
  • It is situated right in the center of...
  • You will find it just north of...
  • It is adjacent to the main entrance...
  • It is positioned along the eastern edge...
  • You can see it directly opposite the...

Official Campus Orientation Guide

Draft for Visiting Medical Staff

[1] Welcome to West Medical University. I have attached a map illustrating the current layout of our campus to help you navigate during your visit.

[2] Overall, the campus is organized around a central hub, with dedicated zones for scientific research, student life, and medical services located near the outer edges. You will notice a large green park bordering the western side.

[3] To help you find your way, start at the Main Medical Library, which is situated directly in the center. Adjacent to the library, to the north, lies the Clinical Research Center where your meetings will be. The eastern side of the campus is dedicated to student life, housing the Student Union and the main dormitory area.

[4] Finally, if you are looking for the Surgical Department, it is located to the south of the central library, near the perimeter. Access to all these facilities is through the Main Gate at the southern end of Main Avenue.

Task 3: Paragraph Purpose

A. Welcoming the visitor and stating the purpose of the email.

B. Giving specific directions starting from a central landmark.

C. Providing directions to the remaining areas and explaining the entrance.

D. Providing a 'Big Picture' so the visitor can mentally map the space.

  • C. Remaining Areas: Paragraph [4]

Task 5: Using Landmarks for Clarity

Vague directions frustrate visitors. Good communicators use central "landmarks" to help people orient themselves. Rewrite these vague sentences into clear, helpful directions based on the map.

Vague: "The Clinical Research Center is over by the trees."
Clearer:
Vague: "Just go find the building for the students."
Clearer:
  • Clearer 1: "The Clinical Research Center is located just north of the Main Medical Library."
  • Clearer 2: "The Student Union and Dormitory are situated on the eastern side of the campus."
